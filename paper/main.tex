\begin{abstract}

The Chern-Simons axion angle $\theta$ is pinned to $0$ or $\pi$ by any symmetry that reverses orientation or time. A lattice vibration that removes all such symmetries makes $\theta$ dynamical, and proposals ranging from axion polaritons to axion dark matter detectors rest on the size of the resulting coupling $\partial \theta/\partial u$. This coupling has so far been a model parameter. Here, we compute it from first principles in two magnetic insulators with distinct topological classifications. \MBT, an antiferromagnetic topological insulator whose ground state has $\theta=\pi$, and the all-in-all-out pyrochlore iridate \YIO, whose ground state is trivial and whose crystal symmetry enforces $\theta=0$. We express the linear response of $\theta$ to an atomic displacement as a Brillouin-zone integral of the trace of a product of non-Abelian Berry curvatures in the joint space of crystal momentum and phonon amplitude, and evaluate it via Wannier interpolation. We then derive the symmetry rules that govern the coupling. A phonon changes $\theta$ at linear order only if it transforms as $\theta$ itself, a one-dimensional representation; it is then infrared-active only if the magnetic point group admits a net magnetization, and never Raman-active when the undistorted crystal is centrosymmetric. In magnets whose magnetic ions sit on inversion centers, no uniform spin fluctuation moves $\theta$ at any order, so the bulk dynamical axion must be a lattice mode. In \YIO{} the three axion-active $\Gamma_2^-$ ($A_{2u}$) phonons, at 229, 328, and 472~\cmi{}, change $\theta$ by $0.010$--$0.024$~rad per {\AA} of displacement. In \MBT{} the axion-active modes are the four zone-boundary $T_2^-$ phonons at 18, 83, 129, and 143~\cmi{}, which changes $\theta$ by 1.4~rad per {\AA}; the largest coupling, 3.6~rad per {\AA}, belongs to the 129~\cmi{} Mn-dominated mode, two orders of magnitude above \YIO{}.


\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\seclab{intro}
%==========================================================================

Many properties of crystalline insulators are governed by the geometry of the occupied Bloch states rather than by the band energies alone~\cite{xiao2010berry, vanderbilt2018berry}. The electric polarization is the most familiar example: it is a Berry phase of the occupied manifold, defined only modulo a quantum~\cite{kingsmith1993theory, resta1994macroscopic}. Its three-dimensional, magnetoelectric counterpart is the Chern-Simons axion angle $\theta$, which describes the isotropic part of the orbital magnetoelectric response~\cite{qi2008topological, essin2009magnetoelectric, essin2010orbital, malashevich2010theory, coh2011chern}. An insulator with $\theta\neq0$ responds to a magnetic field $\mathbf B$ with an electric polarization $\mathbf P=(\theta e^2/2\pi h)\mathbf B$, and to an electric field with the corresponding magnetization; in the language of high-energy physics, $\theta$ plays the role of a background axion field coupled to $\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf B$~\cite{wilczek1987two}.

Like the polarization, $\theta$ is a multivalued quantity, defined modulo $2\pi$. Any symmetry that reverses either spatial orientation or time maps $\theta$ to $-\theta$, and therefore pins it to $0$ or $\pi$~\cite{qi2008topological, essin2009magnetoelectric, mong2010antiferromagnetic, hughes2011inversion, turner2012quantized, marzari2012maximally}. Insulators with $\theta=\pi$ are axion insulators. Time-reversal-symmetric strong topological insulators are the original example, and their quantized magnetoelectric response has been observed through Faraday and Kerr rotation~\cite{wu2016quantized, armitage2019matter}. In magnetic insulators, $\theta=\pi$ can instead be protected by inversion or by a composite of time reversal with a fractional translation~\cite{mong2010antiferromagnetic}. The layered antiferromagnet \MBT{} is the most studied realization~\cite{otrokov2019prediction, li2019intrinsic, zhang2019topological, gong2019experimental}: it is an intrinsic antiferromagnetic topological insulator, whose thin films exhibit quantum anomalous Hall transport~\cite{deng2020quantum}, transport signatures consistent with axion-insulator and Chern-insulator states~\cite{liu2020robust}, and a layer Hall effect~\cite{gao2021layer}.

There is good reason to expect derivatives of $\theta$ to be more tractable than $\theta$ itself.
The history of the polarization is instructive: Born effective charges and dielectric constants, which are first derivatives of $\mathbf P$, were computed before the Berry-phase theory made $\mathbf P$ itself accessible~\cite{kunc1982abinitio, resta1994macroscopic, baroni2001phonons, gonze1997dynamical}. The reason is that $\mathbf P$ is an integral of a gauge-dependent Berry connection and is defined modulo a quantum, whereas $\partial\mathbf P/\partial\lambda$ suffers from neither ambiguity. The same holds for $\theta$. Its Chern-Simons expression requires a smooth, periodic gauge for the occupied states, whose construction and numerical convergence can be delicate~\cite{coh2011chern, liu2015gauge, varnava2020axion, cole2026exact}. For the zero-Chern topological insulators of interest here, such a gauge exists after relaxing the protecting symmetries in the gauge choice; imposing a symmetry-respecting gauge can instead create an obstruction~\cite{soluyanov2011wannier, cole2025reduced}. By contrast, $\partial\theta/\partial\lambda$ is an integral of a gauge-invariant trace of products of Berry curvatures in the four-dimensional space of crystal momentum and $\lambda$~\cite{qi2008topological, essin2010orbital, taherinejad2015adiabatic, olsen2017surface}.

A static, uniform $\theta$ has no effect on bulk electrodynamics. The axion term becomes observable when $\theta$ varies in space or time, producing a Hall current $\propto\nabla\theta\times\mathbf E$ or a current $\propto\dot\theta\,\mathbf B$~\cite{wilczek1987two}. This has motivated a large body of work on \emph{dynamical} axion fields in solids~\cite{nenno2020axion, sekine2021axion}, in which $\theta$ is modulated by a pre-existing degree of freedom of the crystal. Examples include antiferromagnetic fluctuations in magnetically doped topological insulators~\cite{li2010dynamical}, magnetization fluctuations in magnetic topological-insulator superlattices~\cite{wang2016dynamical}, and lattice vibrations that modulate the Dirac mass of a magnetic insulator~\cite{lhachemi2024phononic}. Recently, an oscillation of $\theta$ driven by an antiferromagnetic magnon was reported in a six-septuple-layer film of \MBT{}~\cite{qiu2025observation}. In an applied magnetic field, a dynamical axion mode can hybridize with light to form an axion polariton~\cite{li2010dynamical}. This field-induced mixing underlies proposals to use antiferromagnetic topological insulators as resonant detectors of axion dark matter~\cite{marsh2019proposal, schutteengel2021axion}. In every one of these proposals, the central material parameter is the coupling $\partial\theta/\partial\lambda$ between $\theta$ and the degree of freedom $\lambda$ that drives it. For phonons, however, this coupling has, to our knowledge, only been evaluated within model Hamiltonians.


In this work, we develop a first-principles theory of the coupling between lattice vibrations and the Chern-Simons axion angle. We call a phonon \emph{axion-active} when it changes $\theta$ at first order in its amplitude. We apply the theory to two magnetic insulators with contrasting axion topology: the pyrochlore iridate \YIO{} with all-in-all-out order, which we study in a correlation regime where it is a trivial insulator with $\theta=0$~\cite{wan2011topological, witczak2014correlated}, and the antiferromagnetic topological insulator \MBT{} with $\theta=\pi$. We begin with the symmetry rules. A phonon couples linearly only if it transforms exactly as $\theta$, a one-dimensional representation of the magnetic point group; this is stronger than the commonly stated requirement that it break every quantizing symmetry. Two consequences follow. An axion-active phonon is infrared-active only if the magnetic group admits a net magnetization, and it is never Raman-active when the undistorted crystal has unprimed inversion. In magnets whose magnetic ions occupy inversion centers, which includes both materials studied here, uniform spin fluctuations at fixed ionic positions cannot move $\theta$ along a gapped path, so inversion-breaking phonons provide a bulk dynamical-axion channel that those spin fluctuations lack. From these rules, we then select the axion-active modes and distort the crystal structure according to these patterns. We compute the axion-phonon coupling from fully relativistic density-functional theory (DFT) by evaluating the four-curvature on Wannier-interpolated tight-binding Hamiltonians. The derivatives along the phonon direction are evaluated using finite differences between two independent Wannier models.

\section{The axion angle}
\seclab{theory}
%==========================================================================


The orbital magnetoelectric tensor $\alpha_{ij}=\partial P_i/\partial B_j=\partial M_j/\partial E_i$ of an insulator contains an isotropic, topological part~\cite{qi2008topological, essin2009magnetoelectric, essin2010orbital, malashevich2010theory},
\begin{equation}
\alpha^{\rm CS}_{ij}=\frac{\theta e^2}{2\pi h}\,\delta_{ij},
\eqlab{alpha-CS}
\end{equation}
with $\theta$ given by the Brillouin-zone integral of the Chern-Simons 3-form~\cite{chern1974characteristic},
\begin{equation}
\theta=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\rm BZ}d^3k\,\epsilon^{\mu\nu\sigma}\,\Tr\!\left[\mathcal A_\mu\partial_\nu\mathcal A_\sigma-\frac{2i}{3}\mathcal A_\mu\mathcal A_\nu\mathcal A_\sigma\right],
\eqlab{CS3form}
\end{equation}
where $(\mathcal A_\mu)_{mn}=i\langle u_{m\mathbf k}|\partial_\mu u_{n\mathbf k}\rangle$ is the non-Abelian Berry connection of the occupied states and the trace runs over occupied bands. The integrand of \eq{CS3form} is gauge dependent, and $\theta$ is defined only modulo $2\pi$.
The remaining, non-topological parts of the orbital response~\cite{essin2010orbital, malashevich2010theory}, and the spin and lattice-mediated contributions~\cite{iniguez2008firstprinciples, bousquet2011unexpectedly, malashevich2012full, ye2014dynamical}, are not quantized and are not considered here.


\subsection{Four-curvature formula}
\seclab{fourcurv}

Let $\lambda$ be a parameter of the Hamiltonian along which the gap stays open, such as the amplitude of a lattice displacement, and treat it as a fourth coordinate alongside $\mathbf k$. The connection $\mathcal A_\mu$ then has four
components, $\mu\in\{k_x,k_y,k_z,\lambda\}$, and a covariant curvature $\BB_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu\mathcal A_\nu-\partial_\nu\mathcal A_\mu-i[\mathcal A_\mu,\mathcal A_\nu]$.
Differentiating \eq{CS3form} with respect to $\lambda$, the gauge-dependent terms combine into a total derivative in $\mathbf k$, which integrates to zero over the
closed Brillouin zone. What remains is gauge invariant~\cite{qi2008topological, mong2010antiferromagnetic},
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\lambda}
=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{\rm BZ}d^3k\;\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\,\Tr\!\left[\BB_{\mu\nu}\BB_{\rho\sigma}\right].
\eqlab{4curv}
\end{equation}
Around a closed cycle in $\lambda$, \eq{4curv} integrates to $2\pi C_2$, where
$C_2$ is the second Chern number of the occupied bundle over $(\mathbf k,\lambda)$ ~\cite{essin2009magnetoelectric, taherinejad2015adiabatic}.

Every nonvanishing term in \eq{4curv} carries exactly one $\lambda$ index, and the four placements contribute equally, so that
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\lambda}
=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\rm BZ}d^3k\;\epsilon^{ijl}\,\Tr\!\left[\BB_{ij}\,\BB_{l\lambda}\right],
\eqlab{4curv-reduced}
\end{equation}
with $i,j,l$ the three momentum directions.
In words, the response is the Brillouin-zone integral of the trace of the product of the ordinary (momentum-space) Berry curvature with the mixed momentum-phonon curvature.
Both factors are gauge-covariant, so the integrand is gauge-invariant and no smooth gauge is needed.
Because $\Tr(\BB\BB)\neq\Tr\BB\,\Tr\BB$, the full $N_{\rm occ}\times N_{\rm occ}$ matrices must be kept until the product is formed, where $N_{\rm occ}$ is the number of occupied bands.
This differs from the anomalous Hall conductivity, which involves the band trace of a single curvature~\cite{wang2006abinitio}.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Operations that quantize $\theta$}{Operations that quantize theta}}

Because $\theta$ multiplies $\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf B$, and $\mathbf E$ is a polar, time-even vector while $\mathbf B$ is axial and time-odd, $\theta$ is unchanged by proper rotations and changes sign under improper operations and under time reversal.
For an element $g$ of a magnetic point group with rotation part $R$, the character of $\theta$ is therefore
\begin{equation}
\chi_\theta(g)=\det R\;s(g),
\eqlab{chi-theta}
\end{equation}
where $s(g)=+1$ for unprimed elements and $-1$ for primed elements (those containing $\mathcal T$).
The crystal quantizes $\theta$ if it has any element with $\chi_\theta(g)=-1$; we call such elements $\theta$-odd. They are the unprimed improper operations and the primed proper operations~\cite{varnava2020axion}. In the presence of such symmetries, $\theta$ must be either $\pi$ (topological) or $0$ (trivial).

% \Tref{theta-odd} lists them for the two materials.
% In all-in-all-out \YIO{}, with magnetic point group $m\bar3m'$, inversion is only one of several: the primed fourfold and twofold rotations $C_4'$ and $C_2'$ also quantize $\theta$. In antiferromagnetic \MBT{}, the composite $\Theta=\mathcal T\tau_{1/2}$ of time reversal and the half translation that exchanges the two septuple layers~\cite{mong2010antiferromagnetic} makes the magnetic point group the grey group $\bar3m1'$, in which every primed proper element is $\theta$-odd.

\section{Axion-active phonons}
\seclab{symmetry}

\subsection{Displacements and linear response}
\seclab{notation}

We consider atomic displacements that are periodic in the magnetic unit cell, with $\mathbf u_\kappa$ the displacement of atom $\kappa$ and $\mathbf u$ the collection of all $3N$ Cartesian components. These distortions occur at the center of the magnetic Brillouin zone, although they can originate at a boundary wave vector of the crystallographic Brillouin zone. Assuming that the electronic gap remains open, we choose a continuous branch of the axion angle near the reference structure and expand
\begin{equation}
\theta[\mathbf u]=\theta_0+
\delta\theta^{(1)}[\mathbf u]+\mathcal O(\|\mathbf u\|^2).
\eqlab{theta-expansion}
\end{equation}
The first-order change can be written as
\begin{equation}
\delta\theta^{(1)}[\mathbf u]
=\sum_\kappa\mathbf Z^\theta_\kappa\cdot\mathbf u_\kappa,
\qquad
Z^\theta_{\kappa\alpha}
=\left.\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial u_{\kappa\alpha}}\right|_0.
\eqlab{axion-charge-def}
\end{equation}
We call $\mathbf Z^\theta_\kappa$ the atomic axion charge, by analogy with the Born effective charge and the dynamical magnetic charge~\cite{ye2014dynamical}. It measures the response to displacing atom $\kappa$ and all its magnetic-cell periodic images, with the other atomic coordinates held fixed. It has units of inverse length, reported here in rad/\AA. A rigid translation cannot change $\theta$, so
\begin{equation}
\sum_\kappa\mathbf Z^\theta_\kappa=0.
\eqlab{axion-sum-rule}
\end{equation}
For a displacement pattern $\mathbf u=Q\mathbf v$, the directional derivative is therefore
\begin{equation}
\gamma_{\mathbf v}
\equiv\left.\frac{d\theta[Q\mathbf v]}{dQ}\right|_0
=\sum_\kappa\mathbf Z^\theta_\kappa\cdot\mathbf v_\kappa.
\eqlab{axion-charge}
\end{equation}
A phonon is axion-active when this derivative is nonzero for its normal-mode pattern. Symmetry determines which couplings are allowed; their values require an electronic-response calculation.

\subsection{Linear symmetry selection rule}
\seclab{selection-rule}

Let $\mathcal M$ be the magnetic space group of the reference crystal. An operation $g$ has spatial part $\{R_g|\boldsymbol\tau_g\}$ and may also contain time reversal. Its action on displacements is
\begin{equation}
[D(g)\mathbf u]_{g\kappa}=R_g\mathbf u_\kappa,
\eqlab{displacement-action}
\end{equation}
where $g\kappa$ is the image of atom $\kappa$ modulo a magnetic lattice translation. The translation $\boldsymbol\tau_g$ enters through this permutation of sites; it is not added to the displacement vector. Static displacements are time-even, so time reversal introduces no additional sign. The matrices $D(g)$ are real and orthogonal.

Applying $g$ maps the crystal displaced by $\mathbf u$ onto the crystal displaced by $D(g)\mathbf u$. The axion angle transforms with the character $\chi_\theta(g)=s(g)\det R_g$ of \eq{chi-theta}. Since the reference crystal is invariant under $g$, subtracting its transformation law removes the fixed $2\pi$ offset on the continuous branch:
\begin{equation}
\theta[D(g)\mathbf u]-\theta_0
=\chi_\theta(g)\bigl(\theta[\mathbf u]-\theta_0\bigr).
\eqlab{theta-covariance}
\end{equation}
Keeping the terms linear in $\mathbf u$ gives
\begin{equation}
\delta\theta^{(1)}[D(g)\mathbf u]
=\chi_\theta(g)\delta\theta^{(1)}[\mathbf u].
\eqlab{linear-covariance}
\end{equation}
Thus the transformation of the linear response follows directly from that of $\theta$; it is not an additional assumption. Equivalently, collecting the axion charges into a $3N$-component vector $\mathbf Z^\theta$, \eq{linear-covariance} and the orthogonality of $D(g)$ imply
\begin{equation}
D(g)\mathbf Z^\theta=\chi_\theta(g)\mathbf Z^\theta.
\eqlab{charge-symmetry}
\end{equation}

For a pattern belonging to a one-dimensional irreducible representation (irrep) $\Gamma$, $D(g)\mathbf v=\chi_\Gamma(g)\mathbf v$. Applying \eq{linear-covariance} to this pattern and using linearity yields
\begin{equation}
\bigl[\chi_\Gamma(g)-\chi_\theta(g)\bigr]
\gamma_{\mathbf v}=0.
\eqlab{character-selection}
\end{equation}
A mismatch for even one operation forces the coupling to vanish. A multidimensional irrep cannot support a nonzero linear response either. To couple linearly to $\theta$, a phonon in a single irrep must keep the same displacement pattern under every symmetry, apart from an overall sign change. In a multidimensional irrep, any chosen pattern is transformed by at least one symmetry into a different pattern. It therefore cannot satisfy the linear selection rule. Therefore, the linear response must vanish. Consequently,
\begin{quote}
\emph{A symmetry-adapted phonon can couple linearly to the axion angle only if its displacement pattern transforms as $\theta$ under every magnetic symmetry operation.}
\end{quote}
We denote this one-dimensional representation by $\Gamma_\theta$. Its patterns satisfy
\begin{equation}
D(g)\mathbf v=\chi_\theta(g)\mathbf v
\quad\text{for every }g.
\eqlab{allowed-pattern}
\end{equation}
They reverse under every $\theta$-odd operation and remain unchanged under every $\theta$-even operation. The allowed representation can occur several times. Breaking every quantizing symmetry is therefore necessary, but not sufficient, for a linear coupling. Symmetry does not require the coupling of each resulting phonon to be nonzero.

The analysis can thus be applied systematically: determine the magnetic space-group operations of the reference structure; match their required displacement characters to identify $\Gamma_\theta$; and construct a complete basis of these patterns. The electronic derivatives and force constants in this basis then determine the normal-mode couplings, as described below.

\subsection{Application to \texorpdfstring{\YIO{} and \MBT{}}{Y2Ir2O7 and MnBi2Te4}}
\seclab{application}

Table~\ref{tab:theta-odd} lists the operations that quantize $\theta$ in the two reference structures. Both groups contain unprimed inversion, but inversion is not their only quantizing symmetry.

\paragraph{\YIO{}.}
The all-in-all-out magnetic structure has magnetic space group $Fd\bar3m'$ and a 22-atom primitive cell. Its time-even displacement patterns can be labeled by the irreps of the spatial group $Fd\bar3m$. At $\Gamma$, the required spatial characters equal $\chi_\theta(g)$, because time reversal does not act on a static displacement and lattice translations have unit phase. Fractional translations still enter the mapping of atomic sites. Table~\ref{tab:characters}(a) compares the required characters with the $A_{2u}$ row of the $O_h$ character table, identifying the allowed irrep as $\Gamma_2^-$ in \code{SMODES} notation. In particular, the pattern is even under $C_3$ and the twofold rotations about the cubic axes, but odd under inversion and under the spatial parts of $C_4'$ and $C_{2d}'$. The \code{SMODES} decomposition contains three copies of this irrep, with independent basis patterns on Y, Ir, and O($48f$). The Y and Ir displacements lie along local $\langle111\rangle$ directions. Harmonic coupling can mix these three patterns into three normal modes.

\paragraph{\MBT{}.}
The A-type antiferromagnetic structure has magnetic space group $R_I\bar3c$ and a 14-atom magnetic primitive cell containing two septuple layers. Its antiunitary symmetry $\Theta=\mathcal T\tau_{1/2}$ has $\chi_\theta(\Theta)=-1$. Because static displacements are time-even, \eq{allowed-pattern} requires
\begin{equation}
D(\tau_{1/2})\mathbf v=-\mathbf v.
\eqlab{mbt-antiphase}
\end{equation}
The allowed patterns therefore move neighboring magnetic layers in antiphase. They occur at the parent boundary wave vector $\mathbf q_T=(0,0,3/2)$ in hexagonal reciprocal-lattice coordinates, which folds to magnetic $\Gamma$. The in-phase parent $\Gamma$ sector is excluded already by \eq{mbt-antiphase}.

To assign the parent irrep, we retain the remaining spatial operations as well. For the unitary representatives in Table~\ref{tab:characters}(b), the character of the full phonon operation is the product of the translation factor $\eta_T(g)$ and the parent point-operation character $\chi_{\rm sp}(R_g)$. The selection rule therefore requires
\begin{equation}
\chi_{\rm phonon}(g)
=\eta_T(g)\chi_{\rm sp}(R_g)
=\chi_\theta(g).
\eqlab{mbt-character-match}
\end{equation}
Here $\eta_T=-1$ for a representative carrying $\tau_{1/2}$ and $+1$ for the other listed representatives. For example, the unprimed proper operation $\{C_{2\perp}|\tau_{1/2}\}$ has $\chi_\theta=+1$, so its negative translation factor requires a negative parent $C_{2\perp}$ character. Dividing $\chi_\theta$ by $\eta_T$ gives the required spatial characters in the table. They match the $A_{2u}$ row of $D_{3d}$, identifying the parent space-group irrep as $T_2^-$. The character of the full operation equals $\chi_\theta$; the parent point-operation character need not do so by itself. The \code{SMODES} decomposition contains four copies of $T_2^-$, with basis patterns along $c$ on Mn, Bi, Te1, and Te2, respectively, and opposite displacements on layer partners.

\subsection{From SMODES patterns to phonon couplings}
\seclab{charges}

We use \code{SMODES} from the ISOTROPY suite~\cite{isotropy} to generate a complete set of real symmetry-adapted patterns $\mathbf b_a$, with $a=1,\ldots,n_\theta$, satisfying \eq{allowed-pattern}. Here $n_\theta$ is the multiplicity of the allowed one-dimensional irrep in the complete displacement decomposition: three in \YIO{} and four in \MBT{} (Table~\ref{tab:irreps}). For these time-even coordinates, we use the spatial group obtained from the full magnetic space group by omitting time reversal while preserving every spatial operation and translation. This is $Fd\bar3m$ for \YIO{} and $R\bar3m$ for \MBT{}. The magnetic character condition is then imposed on the resulting patterns. In particular, for \MBT{} we use the parent $T$ sector required by \eq{mbt-antiphase}. The complete \code{SMODES} decomposition supplies all copies of the allowed irrep, and we verify that their basis patterns satisfy the transformation condition under the magnetic operations.

The \code{SMODES} patterns provide basis coordinates, not generally phonon eigenvectors. We retain their Cartesian displacement components as dimensionless vectors, without assuming unit normalization, and write an allowed distortion as
\begin{equation}
\mathbf u_\theta=\sum_{a=1}^{n_\theta}Q_a\mathbf b_a.
\eqlab{symmetry-basis}
\end{equation}
The basis amplitudes $Q_a$ have units of length. We compute one electronic-response derivative for each basis direction using \eq{4curv},
\begin{equation}
\left.\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial Q_a}\right|_0
=\sum_\kappa\mathbf Z^\theta_\kappa\cdot\mathbf b_{a\kappa}.
\eqlab{basis-response}
\end{equation}
Linearity then gives the response to any displacement in the allowed sector,
\begin{equation}
\delta\theta^{(1)}[\mathbf u_\theta]
=\sum_{a=1}^{n_\theta}\left.\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial Q_a}\right|_0Q_a.
\eqlab{basis-reconstruction}
\end{equation}
The linear selection rule makes the response vanish on all other symmetry sectors. This is the justification for evaluating the response along symmetry-adapted patterns: the $n_\theta$ derivatives determine the linear response of every displacement in the allowed sector, independently of the choice of basis.

The physical phonons are determined by the harmonic force constants. With $E$ the energy per magnetic unit cell, define
\begin{equation}
\Phi_{\kappa\alpha,\kappa'\beta}
=\left.\frac{\partial^2 E}
{\partial u_{\kappa\alpha}\partial u_{\kappa'\beta}}\right|_0,
\qquad
\mathcal D=M^{-1/2}\Phi M^{-1/2},
\eqlab{dynmat}
\end{equation}
where $M$ is diagonal in the atomic Cartesian basis, with entries $m_\kappa$. The mass-weighted normal modes satisfy $\mathcal D\mathbf e_n=\omega_n^2\mathbf e_n$. The force constants and masses respect the magnetic symmetries, so the allowed sector is closed under the harmonic equations of motion. Only its $n_\theta\times n_\theta$ block is required.

In the symmetry basis, this block is specified by the stiffness and mass matrices
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf K_{ab}
&=\sum_{\kappa\alpha,\kappa'\beta}
b_{a,\kappa\alpha}\Phi_{\kappa\alpha,\kappa'\beta}
b_{b,\kappa'\beta},\\
\mathsf M_{ab}
&=\sum_\kappa m_\kappa
\mathbf b_{a\kappa}\cdot\mathbf b_{b\kappa}.
\end{aligned}
\eqlab{reduced-matrices}
\end{equation}
We obtain the stiffness matrix from the forces induced by displacing along each basis pattern,
\begin{equation}
\mathsf K_{ab}
=-\sum_\kappa\mathbf b_{a\kappa}\cdot
\left.\frac{\partial\mathbf F_\kappa}{\partial Q_b}\right|_0.
\eqlab{projected-forces}
\end{equation}
These force responses supply the off-diagonal elements as well as the diagonal ones; the latter alone would not determine the normal modes. No orthogonality or mass normalization of the \code{SMODES} basis is assumed.

The normal-mode coefficients solve the generalized eigenvalue problem
\begin{equation}
\mathsf K\mathbf c_n=\omega_n^2\mathsf M\mathbf c_n,
\qquad
\mathbf c_n^T\mathsf M\mathbf c_m=\delta_{nm}.
\eqlab{reduced-eigenproblem}
\end{equation}
The corresponding physical displacement is $\mathbf u=q_n\mathbf v_n$, where
\begin{equation}
\mathbf v_{n\kappa}=\sum_a c_{an}\mathbf b_{a\kappa}
=\frac{\mathbf e_{\kappa n}}{\sqrt{m_\kappa}},
\qquad
\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial q_n}
=\sum_a c_{an} \left.\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial Q_a}\right .
\eqlab{mode-coupling}
\end{equation}
Here $q_n$ is a mass-weighted normal coordinate, with units of length times the square root of mass. The electronic derivatives and force-constant block must use the same basis normalization. Their combination gives the phonon frequencies and axion couplings without requiring a separate electronic-response calculation along each final normal-mode pattern. For \YIO{} and \MBT{}, this requires three and four basis derivatives, respectively, together with stiffness matrices of order three and four.

\subsection{Optical activity and magnetic fluctuations}

The same transformation rule constrains optical activity. A mode is allowed in ordinary electric-dipole infrared absorption if it transforms as a component of a polar, time-even vector. The product of this vector representation with $\Gamma_\theta$ has matrices
\begin{equation}
\chi_\theta(g)R_g=s(g)\det R_g\,R_g,
\eqlab{magnetization-representation}
\end{equation}
which are exactly those of a time-odd axial vector, such as magnetization. Thus a linear axion coupling and an electric-dipole infrared coupling can coexist only if the magnetic point group allows a net magnetization component. Both reference point groups forbid net magnetization, so their axion-active phonons are infrared-silent. Moreover, unprimed inversion makes these phonons odd, while the electric-dipole Raman tensor is inversion-even. Their ordinary first-order Raman coupling also vanishes. The axion-active sectors in Table~\ref{tab:irreps} are therefore silent in both channels. These restrictions concern the reference magnetic groups and do not imply optical silence for axion-active phonons in every magnet.

Finally, the location of the ordered magnetic moments distinguishes the lattice and spin channels in these materials. One common unprimed inversion maps every Ir site in \YIO{}, or every Mn site in \MBT{}, onto itself modulo a magnetic lattice translation. Spins are axial vectors and are unchanged by inversion. Consequently, any configuration of these local moments that is periodic in the magnetic cell preserves this inversion when the ionic positions are fixed. Within a connected insulating phase with vanishing bulk Chern numbers, $\theta$ remains quantized and cannot change along such a spin deformation that keeps the gap open~\cite{turner2012quantized,zhang2020large,sekine2021axion}. The obstruction applies beyond linear order to these fixed-site spin degrees of freedom. It does not exclude finite-wave-vector spin excitations, films, or additional inversion-breaking electronic order. The inversion-breaking phonon sectors identified above provide a bulk dynamical-axion channel that these magnetic-cell-periodic spin fluctuations cannot supply.

\section{MnBi$_2$Te$_4$}
\seclab{MBT}
%==========================================================================

\subsection{Crystal and magnetic structure}

MnBi$_2$Te$_4$ (MBT) crystallizes in a rhombohedral structure with space group $R\bar{3}m$ (No.~166).
The structure consists of septuple layers Te-Bi-Te-Mn-Te-Bi-Te stacked along the crystallographic $c$ axis.
The Mn ions adopt a $2+$ valence with spin $S = 5/2$ and a large magnetic moment of approximately $5\,\mu_\text{B}$.
Below the N\'eel temperature $T_N = 24$~K, the Mn$^{2+}$ moments order ferromagnetically within the $ab$ plane but antiferromagnetically along the $c$ axis in an A-type antiferromagnetic (AFM) arrangement \cite{Yan2019}.

In the magnetically ordered state, time-reversal symmetry $\mathcal{T}$ is broken.
However, the system retains spatial inversion $\mathcal{P}$ (centered at the Mn site) as well as the combined antiunitary symmetry $\Theta = \mathcal{T} S_{1/2}$, consisting of time reversal followed by a half-lattice translation along the $c$ axis \cite{mong2010}.
This half-translation maps Mn$^{2+}$ ions with spin-up to those with spin-down, so that $\Theta$ is preserved despite the magnetic order.
Together, $\mathcal{P}$ and $\Theta$ enforce the quantization $\theta = \pi$, making MBT both an antiferromagnetic topological insulator and an axion insulator \cite{Turner2012}.

At the surface, time-reversal symmetry is broken by the AFM ordering, leading to gapped surface Dirac modes and a half-quantized surface anomalous Hall conductivity.

\subsection{Symmetry analysis and identification of axion-active modes}

To induce a first-order change in $\theta$, a phonon mode must break both $\mathcal{P}$ and $\Theta$.
Because $\Theta$ involves a half-translation along $c$, phonons at finite crystal momentum along $k_z$ are natural candidates for breaking this symmetry.
We therefore focus on phonons at the $Z$ point of the hexagonal Brillouin zone of the non-magnetic crystal structure, corresponding to wavevector $(0,0,\pi/c)$.

Using the \code{SMODES} software package, we enumerate the irreducible representations of the $Z$-point little group and identify displacement patterns that break the relevant symmetries.
Among all irreps at $Z$, the $T_2^-$ representation simultaneously breaks inversion $\mathcal{P}$ and, in the magnetic system, the $\Theta$ symmetry.
There are four distinct modes transforming under $T_2^-$; these are the axion-active phonons of MBT.

The displacement patterns of the four $T_2^-$ modes involve different subsets of atoms in the septuple layer.
Mode~1 is dominated by Mn displacements, mode~2 involves primarily Bi atoms, mode~3 displaces the inner Te atoms, and mode~4 involves the outer Te atoms.
Because these are zone-boundary modes (at $Z$), the atomic displacements alternate in sign between adjacent unit cells along $c$, ensuring that both inversion and $\Theta$ are broken.

\subsection{Computational details for MBT}

The first-principles calculations for MBT are performed using \code{Quantum ESPRESSO} with fully relativistic PAW pseudopotentials incorporating spin-orbit coupling.
The A-type AFM ordering is imposed via noncollinear magnetism, with the Mn$_1$ and Mn$_2$ sublattices initialized with opposite spin orientations along the $c$ axis.
We employ a plane-wave energy cutoff of 60~Ry (charge density cutoff of 240~Ry) and include van der Waals corrections via the DFT-D3 scheme to properly describe the interlayer bonding.
Calculations are performed with and without a Hubbard $U$ correction on the Mn $3d$ orbitals; we test values of $U = 0$, 1, 3, and 4~eV.

For each axion-active phonon mode, atomic positions are displaced along the normalized \code{SMODES} eigenvector with an amplitude $Q = 0.01$~\AA, chosen to remain within the linear-response regime.
Both the undistorted and displaced structures are converged self-consistently, followed by non-self-consistent (NSCF) calculations on a uniform $k$-point mesh.

The resulting Bloch eigenstates are Wannierized using \code{Wannier90} \cite{marzari1997, marzari2012} with a disentanglement window of $-7$ to $15$~eV and a frozen window of $2.5$ to $11$~eV.
The initial projections consist of Mn $3d$, Bi $6p$, and Te $5p$ orbitals on all atoms in the unit cell, yielding 92 spinor trial states that span 46 Wannier functions (after accounting for spin-orbit coupling) from a set of approximately 75 entangled bands.
The Wannierized tight-binding Hamiltonians are imported into the \code{PythTB} framework \cite{Cole_Python_Tight_Binding_2025} for efficient evaluation of the axion response.

An important technical consideration concerns the $\mathcal{P}\Theta$ symmetry, which enforces Kramers-like degeneracy at every $\mathbf{k}$-point even in the magnetically ordered state.
\code{Quantum ESPRESSO} does not automatically detect this composite symmetry; to enforce the correct degeneracy structure we shift the crystallographic origin to the midpoint between the two Mn sublattices.

\subsection{Results for MBT}

The axion response $\partial_Q \theta$ is computed for each of the four $T_2^-$ modes using \eq{4curv}.
We perform a systematic convergence study as a function of $k$-mesh density, evaluating the integral on uniform meshes ranging from $4 \times 4 \times 4$ to $40 \times 40 \times 40$ points.
The minimum direct band gap of MBT is small (approximately 47~meV at the $Z$ point), requiring dense $k$-meshes for convergence.

For mode~2 of the $T_2^-$ irrep (the Bi-dominated mode) at displacement amplitude $Q = 0.01$~\AA, we obtain a converged value of $\partial_Q \theta \approx 10.0$~rad/\AA\ on the finest meshes.
The convergence behavior is smooth, with the result stabilizing beyond approximately $20 \times 20 \times 20$ $k$-points.

\section{Y$_2$Ir$_2$O$_7$}
\seclab{YIO}
%==========================================================================

\subsection{Crystal and magnetic structure}

Y$_2$Ir$_2$O$_7$ (YIO) is a pyrochlore iridate crystallizing in the cubic $Fd\bar{3}m$ space group (No.~227), with conventional lattice parameter $a = 10.19$~\AA\ \cite{wan2011topological, witczak2014correlated}.
The primitive cell contains 22 atoms: 4~Y at the $16d$ Wyckoff position, 4~Ir at $16c$, 2~O at the $8b$ site, and 12~O at the $48f$ site.
The Ir$^{4+}$ ions ($5d^5$, effective total angular momentum $J_\text{eff} = 1/2$) reside on a pyrochlore lattice---a network of corner-sharing tetrahedra---and form corner-sharing IrO$_6$ octahedra with a tilt angle of approximately $53^\circ$.

Below approximately $T_N \approx 150$~K, the Ir moments order in an all-in-all-out (AIAO) magnetic structure, in which the four Ir moments on each tetrahedron point either all toward or all away from the tetrahedral center \cite{wan2011topological, pesin2010mott}. The AIAO ordering direction on each Ir sublattice points along the local $\langle 111 \rangle$ axis of the pyrochlore lattice. This magnetic order breaks time-reversal symmetry $\mathcal{T}$ but preserves spatial inversion $\mathcal{P}$, which maps each ``all-in'' tetrahedron to an ``all-out'' tetrahedron and vice versa. 

The phase realized by Y$_2$Ir$_2$O$_7$ with AIAO order depends sensitively on the strength of electronic correlations.
Theoretical calculations predict a Weyl semimetal at weak correlations, a topological axion insulator with $\theta = \pi$ at intermediate correlations, and a trivial Mott insulator with $\theta = 0$ at strong correlations \cite{wan2011topological, witczak2014correlated}. In this work, we study YIO in a parameter regime (Hubbard $U = 1$--$3$~eV on Ir $5d$) where the system is gapped and in the trivial phase with $\theta = 0$.
This choice provides a non-topological counterpoint to MBT, allowing us to contrast the phonon-induced departure of $\theta$ from $0$ (in YIO) with the departure from $\pi$ (in MBT).
The system exhibits a total magnetization of approximately $2\,\mu_\text{B}$ per formula unit arising from the AIAO-ordered Ir moments.

\section{Computational methodology}
\seclab{methods}
%==========================================================================


\subsection{Density-functional theory calculations}

All density-functional theory calculations are performed using the \code{Quantum ESPRESSO} package with fully relativistic projector augmented-wave (PAW) pseudopotentials, noncollinear magnetism, and spin-orbit coupling.
The exchange-correlation functional is treated within the \st{PBE generalized gradient approximation} LDA.

A Hubbard $U$ correction is applied to the transition-metal $d$ states to account for on-site Coulomb repulsion; the optimal value of $U$ is determined by comparison with experimental band gaps and magnetic moments.

For each material, we perform self-consistent (SCF) calculations for the undistorted structure and for structures displaced along each axion-active phonon mode.
Non-self-consistent (NSCF) calculations are then performed on a uniform $k$-point mesh to obtain the Bloch eigenstates needed for Wannierization.


\subsection{Computational details for YIO}

The first-principles calculations for Y$_2$Ir$_2$O$_7$ are performed using \code{Quantum ESPRESSO} with fully relativistic PAW pseudopotentials and spin-orbit coupling.
The AIAO magnetic order is imposed by initializing the four Ir sublattice moments along their respective local $\langle 111 \rangle$ directions.
The four Ir sites are treated as distinct atomic types in the \code{Quantum ESPRESSO} input to allow independent specification of the magnetic moment directions.
We use a plane-wave energy cutoff of 50~Ry (charge density cutoff of 500~Ry) and a $4 \times 4 \times 4$ $k$-point mesh for the self-consistent calculation.
A Hubbard $U$ correction is applied to the Ir $5d$ states within the ortho-atomic DFT+$U$ framework; we test values of $U = 1.0$ and $3.0$~eV.
Marzari-Vanderbilt cold smearing with a broadening of 5~mRy is used to stabilize the self-consistent cycle.

For each $\Gamma_2^-$ phonon mode, we displace the atomic positions by an amplitude of $Q = 0.01$~\AA\ along the normalized \code{SMODES} eigenvector.
The electronic structure of both undistorted and displaced configurations is then Wannierized and the axion response computed following the same four-curvature procedure used for MBT.

\subsection{Wannierization}

Starting from the NSCF Bloch eigenstates, we construct maximally localized Wannier functions using \code{Wannier90}.
The disentanglement procedure is employed to extract a set of smoothly connected bands from the entangled manifold.
Initial projections are chosen based on the atomic orbital character of the relevant bands and the resulting tight-binding Hamiltonians faithfully reproduce the low-energy electronic structure.

For MBT, the Wannier basis consists of 92 spinor trial states (Mn $3d$, Bi $6p$, Te $5p$) spanning 46 Wannier functions with a disentanglement window of $[-7, 15]$~eV and a frozen window of $[2.5, 11]$~eV.
The Wannierized Hamiltonians are imported into the \code{PythTB} framework \cite{Cole_Python_Tight_Binding_2025}.
