\begin{abstract}

The Chern-Simons axion angle $\theta$ is pinned to $0$ or $\pi$ by any symmetry that reverses orientation or time. A lattice vibration that removes all such symmetries makes $\theta$ dynamical, and proposals ranging from axion polaritons to axion dark matter detectors rest on the size of the resulting coupling $\partial \theta/\partial u$. This coupling has so far been a model parameter. Here, we compute it from first principles in two magnetic insulators with distinct topological classifications. \MBT, an antiferromagnetic topological insulator whose ground state has $\theta=\pi$, and the all-in-all-out pyrochlore iridate \YIO, whose ground state is trivial and whose crystal symmetry enforces $\theta=0$. We express the linear response of $\theta$ to an atomic displacement as a Brillouin-zone integral of the trace of a product of non-Abelian Berry curvatures in the joint space of crystal momentum and phonon amplitude, and evaluate it via Wannier interpolation. We then derive the symmetry rules that govern the coupling. A phonon changes $\theta$ at linear order only if it transforms as $\theta$ itself, a one-dimensional representation; it is then infrared-active only if the magnetic point group admits a net magnetization, and never Raman-active when the undistorted crystal is centrosymmetric. In magnets whose magnetic ions sit on inversion centers, no uniform spin fluctuation moves $\theta$ at any order, so the bulk dynamical axion must be a lattice mode. In \YIO{} the three axion-active $\Gamma_2^-$ ($A_{2u}$) phonons, at 229, 328, and 472~\cmi{}, change $\theta$ by $0.010$--$0.024$~rad per {\AA} of displacement. In \MBT{} the axion-active modes are the four zone-boundary $T_2^-$ phonons at 18, 83, 129, and 143~\cmi{}, which changes $\theta$ by 1.4~rad per {\AA}; the largest coupling, 3.6~rad per {\AA}, belongs to the 129~\cmi{} Mn-dominated mode, two orders of magnitude above \YIO{}.


\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\seclab{intro}
%==========================================================================

Many properties of crystalline insulators are governed by the geometry of the occupied Bloch states rather than by the band energies alone~\cite{xiao2010berry, vanderbilt2018berry}. The electric polarization is the most familiar example: it is a Berry phase of the occupied manifold, defined only modulo a quantum~\cite{kingsmith1993theory, resta1994macroscopic}. Its three-dimensional, magnetoelectric counterpart is the Chern-Simons axion angle $\theta$, which describes the isotropic part of the orbital magnetoelectric response~\cite{qi2008topological, essin2009magnetoelectric, essin2010orbital, malashevich2010theory, coh2011chern}. An insulator with $\theta\neq0$ responds to a magnetic field $\mathbf B$ with an electric polarization $\mathbf P=(\theta e^2/2\pi h)\mathbf B$, and to an electric field with the corresponding magnetization; in the language of high-energy physics, $\theta$ plays the role of a background axion field coupled to $\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf B$~\cite{wilczek1987two}.

Like the polarization, $\theta$ is a multivalued quantity, defined modulo $2\pi$. Any symmetry that reverses either spatial orientation or time maps $\theta$ to $-\theta$, and therefore pins it to $0$ or $\pi$~\cite{qi2008topological, essin2009magnetoelectric, mong2010antiferromagnetic, hughes2011inversion, turner2012quantized, marzari2012maximally}. Insulators with $\theta=\pi$ are axion insulators. Time-reversal-symmetric strong topological insulators are the original example, and their quantized magnetoelectric response has been observed through Faraday and Kerr rotation~\cite{wu2016quantized, armitage2019matter}. In magnetic insulators, $\theta=\pi$ can instead be protected by inversion or by a composite of time reversal with a fractional translation~\cite{mong2010antiferromagnetic}. The layered antiferromagnet \MBT{} is the most studied realization~\cite{otrokov2019prediction, li2019intrinsic, zhang2019topological, gong2019experimental}: it is an intrinsic antiferromagnetic topological insulator, whose thin films exhibit quantum anomalous Hall transport~\cite{deng2020quantum}, transport signatures consistent with axion-insulator and Chern-insulator states~\cite{liu2020robust}, and a layer Hall effect~\cite{gao2021layer}.

There is good reason to expect derivatives of $\theta$ to be more tractable than $\theta$ itself.
The history of the polarization is instructive: Born effective charges and dielectric constants, which are first derivatives of $\mathbf P$, were computed before the Berry-phase theory made $\mathbf P$ itself accessible~\cite{kunc1982abinitio, resta1994macroscopic, baroni2001phonons, gonze1997dynamical}. The reason is that $\mathbf P$ is an integral of a gauge-dependent Berry connection and is defined modulo a quantum, whereas $\partial\mathbf P/\partial\lambda$ suffers from neither ambiguity. The same holds for $\theta$. Its Chern-Simons expression requires a smooth, periodic gauge for the occupied states, whose construction and numerical convergence can be delicate~\cite{coh2011chern, liu2015gauge, varnava2020axion, cole2026exact}. For the zero-Chern topological insulators of interest here, such a gauge exists after relaxing the protecting symmetries in the gauge choice; imposing a symmetry-respecting gauge can instead create an obstruction~\cite{soluyanov2011wannier, cole2025reduced}. By contrast, $\partial\theta/\partial\lambda$ is an integral of a gauge-invariant trace of products of Berry curvatures in the four-dimensional space of crystal momentum and $\lambda$~\cite{qi2008topological, essin2010orbital, taherinejad2015adiabatic, olsen2017surface}.

A static, uniform $\theta$ has no effect on bulk electrodynamics. The axion term becomes observable when $\theta$ varies in space or time, producing a Hall current $\propto\nabla\theta\times\mathbf E$ or a current $\propto\dot\theta\,\mathbf B$~\cite{wilczek1987two}. This has motivated a large body of work on \emph{dynamical} axion fields in solids~\cite{nenno2020axion, sekine2021axion}, in which $\theta$ is modulated by a pre-existing degree of freedom of the crystal. Examples include antiferromagnetic fluctuations in magnetically doped topological insulators~\cite{li2010dynamical}, magnetization fluctuations in magnetic topological-insulator superlattices~\cite{wang2016dynamical}, and lattice vibrations that modulate the Dirac mass of a magnetic insulator~\cite{lhachemi2024phononic}. Recently, an oscillation of $\theta$ driven by an antiferromagnetic magnon was reported in a six-septuple-layer film of \MBT{}~\cite{qiu2025observation}. In an applied magnetic field, a dynamical axion mode can hybridize with light to form an axion polariton~\cite{li2010dynamical}. This field-induced mixing underlies proposals to use antiferromagnetic topological insulators as resonant detectors of axion dark matter~\cite{marsh2019proposal, schutteengel2021axion}. In every one of these proposals, the central material parameter is the coupling $\partial\theta/\partial\lambda$ between $\theta$ and the degree of freedom $\lambda$ that drives it. For phonons, however, this coupling has, to our knowledge, only been evaluated within model Hamiltonians.


In this work, we develop a first-principles theory of the coupling between lattice vibrations and the Chern-Simons axion angle. We call a phonon \emph{axion-active} when it changes $\theta$ at first order in its amplitude.
% We begin with the symmetry rules. A phonon couples linearly only if it transforms exactly as $\theta$, a one-dimensional representation of the magnetic point group; this is stronger than the commonly stated requirement that it break every quantizing symmetry. Two consequences follow. An axion-active phonon is infrared-active only if the magnetic group admits a net magnetization, and it is never Raman-active when the undistorted crystal has unprimed inversion. In magnets whose magnetic ions occupy inversion centers, which includes both materials studied here, uniform spin fluctuations at fixed ionic positions cannot move $\theta$ along a gapped path, so inversion-breaking phonons provide a bulk dynamical-axion channel that those spin fluctuations lack.
We apply the theory to two magnetic insulators with contrasting axion topology: the pyrochlore iridate \YIO{} with all-in-all-out order, which we study in a correlation regime where it is a trivial insulator with $\theta=0$~\cite{wan2011topological, witczak2014correlated}, and the antiferromagnetic topological insulator \MBT{} with $\theta=\pi$. We first identify the symmetry conditions that allow phonons to couple linearly to the axion angle in each magnetic space group. From these rules, we then select the axion-active modes and distort the crystal structure according to these patterns. We compute the axion-phonon coupling from fully relativistic density-functional theory (DFT) by evaluating the four-curvature on Wannier-interpolated tight-binding Hamiltonians. The derivatives along the phonon direction are evaluated using finite differences between two independent Wannier models.

\section{The axion angle}
\seclab{theory}
%==========================================================================


The orbital magnetoelectric tensor $\alpha_{ij}=\partial P_i/\partial B_j=\partial M_j/\partial E_i$ of an insulator contains an isotropic, topological part~\cite{qi2008topological, essin2009magnetoelectric, essin2010orbital, malashevich2010theory},
\begin{equation}
\alpha^{\rm CS}_{ij}=\frac{\theta e^2}{2\pi h}\,\delta_{ij},
\eqlab{alpha-CS}
\end{equation}
with $\theta$ given by the Brillouin-zone integral of the Chern-Simons 3-form~\cite{chern1974characteristic},
\begin{equation}
\theta=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\rm BZ}d^3k\,\epsilon^{\mu\nu\sigma}\,\Tr\!\left[\mathcal A_\mu\partial_\nu\mathcal A_\sigma-\frac{2i}{3}\mathcal A_\mu\mathcal A_\nu\mathcal A_\sigma\right],
\eqlab{CS3form}
\end{equation}
where $(\mathcal A_\mu)_{mn}=i\langle u_{m\mathbf k}|\partial_\mu u_{n\mathbf k}\rangle$ is the non-Abelian Berry connection of the occupied states and the trace runs over occupied bands. The integrand of \eq{CS3form} is gauge dependent, and $\theta$ is defined only modulo $2\pi$.
The remaining, non-topological parts of the orbital response~\cite{essin2010orbital, malashevich2010theory}, and the spin and lattice-mediated contributions~\cite{iniguez2008firstprinciples, bousquet2011unexpectedly, malashevich2012full, ye2014dynamical}, are not quantized and are not considered here.


\subsection{Four-curvature formula}
\seclab{fourcurv}

Let $\lambda$ be a parameter of the Hamiltonian along which the gap stays open, such as the amplitude of a lattice displacement, and treat it as a fourth coordinate alongside $\mathbf k$. The connection $\mathcal A_\mu$ then has four
components, $\mu\in\{k_x,k_y,k_z,\lambda\}$, and a covariant curvature $\BB_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu\mathcal A_\nu-\partial_\nu\mathcal A_\mu-i[\mathcal A_\mu,\mathcal A_\nu]$.
Differentiating \eq{CS3form} with respect to $\lambda$, the gauge-dependent terms combine into a total derivative in $\mathbf k$, which integrates to zero over the
closed Brillouin zone. What remains is gauge invariant~\cite{qi2008topological, mong2010antiferromagnetic},
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\lambda}
=\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{\rm BZ}d^3k\;\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\,\Tr\!\left[\BB_{\mu\nu}\BB_{\rho\sigma}\right].
\eqlab{4curv}
\end{equation}
Around a closed cycle in $\lambda$, \eq{4curv} integrates to $2\pi C_2$, where
$C_2$ is the second Chern number of the occupied bundle over $(\mathbf k,\lambda)$ ~\cite{essin2009magnetoelectric, taherinejad2015adiabatic}.

Every nonvanishing term in \eq{4curv} carries exactly one $\lambda$ index, and the four placements contribute equally, so that
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\lambda}
=\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{\rm BZ}d^3k\;\epsilon^{ijl}\,\Tr\!\left[\BB_{ij}\,\BB_{l\lambda}\right],
\eqlab{4curv-reduced}
\end{equation}
with $i,j,l$ the three momentum directions.
In words, the response is the Brillouin-zone integral of the trace of the product of the ordinary (momentum-space) Berry curvature with the mixed momentum-phonon curvature.
Both factors are gauge-covariant, so the integrand is gauge-invariant and no smooth gauge is needed.
Because $\Tr(\BB\BB)\neq\Tr\BB\,\Tr\BB$, the full $N_{\rm occ}\times N_{\rm occ}$ matrices must be kept until the product is formed, where $N_{\rm occ}$ is the number of occupied bands.
This differs from the anomalous Hall conductivity, which involves the band trace of a single curvature~\cite{wang2006abinitio}.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{Operations that quantize $\theta$}{Operations that quantize theta}}

Because $\theta$ multiplies $\mathbf E\cdot\mathbf B$, and $\mathbf E$ is a polar, time-even vector while $\mathbf B$ is axial and time-odd, $\theta$ is unchanged by proper rotations and changes sign under improper operations and under time reversal.
For an element $g$ of a magnetic point group with rotation part $R$, the character of $\theta$ is therefore
\begin{equation}
\chi_\theta(g)=\det R\;s(g),
\eqlab{chi-theta}
\end{equation}
where $s(g)=+1$ for unprimed elements and $-1$ for primed elements (those containing $\mathcal T$).
The crystal quantizes $\theta$ if it has any element with $\chi_\theta(g)=-1$; we call such elements $\theta$-odd. They are the unprimed improper operations and the primed proper operations~\cite{varnava2020axion}. In the presence of such symmetries, $\theta$ must be either $\pi$ (topological) or $0$ (trivial).

% \Tref{theta-odd} lists them for the two materials.
% In all-in-all-out \YIO{}, with magnetic point group $m\bar3m'$, inversion is only one of several: the primed fourfold and twofold rotations $C_4'$ and $C_2'$ also quantize $\theta$. In antiferromagnetic \MBT{}, the composite $\Theta=\mathcal T\tau_{1/2}$ of time reversal and the half translation that exchanges the two septuple layers~\cite{mong2010antiferromagnetic} makes the magnetic point group the grey group $\bar3m1'$, in which every primed proper element is $\theta$-odd.

\section{Axion-active phonons}
\seclab{symmetry}
%==========================================================================

We consider lattice distortions that are periodic in the magnetic unit cell. The displacement of atom $\kappa$ from its reference position is denoted by $\mathbf u_\kappa$, and the collection of atomic displacements by $\mathbf u=\{\mathbf u_\kappa\}$. These distortions have zero wave vector in the magnetic Brillouin zone, although they need not be zone-center distortions of the crystallographic primitive cell. Assuming that the electronic gap remains open, we expand the axion angle about the undistorted structure as
\begin{equation}
\theta(\mathbf u)
=\theta_0+\delta\theta^{(1)}[\mathbf u]
+\mathcal O(\|\mathbf u\|^2),
\end{equation}
where $\delta\theta^{(1)}$ is linear in the atomic displacements. The expansion is understood on a continuous branch of $\theta$ near the reference structure; its derivatives are independent of the $2\pi$ branch choice. We call a phonon axion-active when its displacement pattern produces a nonzero first-order change in $\theta$.

The linear response can be expressed in terms of atom-resolved coefficients. The familiar Born effective charge tensor,
\[
Z^{*}_{\kappa,\alpha\beta}
=\frac{\Omega}{e}
\frac{\partial P_\alpha}{\partial u_{\kappa\beta}},
\]
describes the change in electric polarization induced by displacing an atomic sublattice, where \(\Omega\) is the cell volume and \(e\) the elementary charge. Analogously, we define the atomic axion charge
\[
\mathbf Z^\theta_\kappa
=\left.\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\mathbf u_\kappa}\right|_{\mathbf u=0},
\]
so that
\[
\delta\theta^{(1)}[\mathbf u]
=\sum_\kappa\mathbf Z^\theta_\kappa\cdot\mathbf u_\kappa.
\]
Each derivative corresponds to displacing atom \(\kappa\) and its magnetic-cell periodic images while holding the other atomic coordinates fixed. Since \(\theta\) is a scalar, the axion charge has one Cartesian index, whereas the Born effective charge has two. With this definition, \(\mathbf Z^\theta_\kappa\) has units of inverse length, which we report in rad/\(\text{\AA}\).

\subsection{Linear selection rule}
The symmetries of the undistorted magnetic crystal constrain this linear response. Let \(g\) be an element of its magnetic space group, with spatial part \(\{R_g|\boldsymbol\tau_g\}\). If \(g\) maps reference atom \(\kappa\) onto atom \(g\kappa\), modulo a magnetic lattice translation, its action on the displacement pattern is
\[
[D(g)\mathbf u]_{g\kappa}=R_g\mathbf u_\kappa.
\]
Thus \(D(g)\) rotates each displacement vector and assigns it to the image of its atomic site. The translation \(\boldsymbol\tau_g\) enters through this mapping of sites; it is not added to the displacement itself. Displacements are time-even, so time reversal introduces no additional sign. Since \(D(g)\) combines a spatial rotation with a permutation of atomic labels, it is a real orthogonal matrix.

The transformation law can be applied directly to the response of a frozen displacement pattern. For a distortion \(\mathbf u_\kappa=Q\mathbf v_\kappa\), define
\[
\gamma_{\mathbf v}
=\left.\frac{d\theta(Q\mathbf v)}{dQ}\right|_{Q=0}
=\sum_\kappa\mathbf Z^\theta_\kappa\cdot\mathbf v_\kappa.
\]
Suppose that \(\mathbf v\) belongs to a one-dimensional irrep \(\Gamma\), so that
\[
D(g)\mathbf v=\chi_\Gamma(g)\mathbf v.
\]
A symmetry operation therefore maps the frozen structure at amplitude \(Q\) onto the structure at amplitude \(\chi_\Gamma(g)Q\).

There are two ways to express the response along this transformed pattern. The transformation law of the axion angle requires
\[
\gamma_{D(g)\mathbf v}
=\chi_\theta(g)\gamma_{\mathbf v}.
\]
Meanwhile, the linearity of the response in the displacement direction gives
\[
\gamma_{D(g)\mathbf v}
=\gamma_{\chi_\Gamma(g)\mathbf v}
=\chi_\Gamma(g)\gamma_{\mathbf v}.
\]
Equating these expressions yields
\[
[\chi_\Gamma(g)-\chi_\theta(g)]\gamma_{\mathbf v}=0.
\]
Thus a mismatch between the two characters for even one magnetic symmetry operation forces the linear coupling to vanish. A nonzero coupling is allowed only if
\[
\chi_\Gamma(g)=\chi_\theta(g)
\qquad\text{for every }g.
\]
In particular, the pattern must reverse under every operation that reverses \(\theta\), and remain unchanged under every operation that preserves \(\theta\).

For a multidimensional irrep, symmetry operations mix several partner patterns rather than simply preserving or reversing one pattern. A nonzero linear response would single out a combination of their amplitudes that transforms independently according to the one-dimensional character \(\chi_\theta\). Such an independently transforming component cannot occur within a multidimensional irreducible representation. Consequently, only copies of the one-dimensional representation \(\chi_\theta\) can contribute to the linear axion response.

This identifies the symmetry-allowed displacement sector before evaluating its coupling. The representation may occur several times, giving several independent patterns whose frozen-displacement responses must be calculated. Matching the required symmetry permits these responses to be nonzero but does not determine their values.

Applying \(g\) to the distorted crystal produces the crystal with displacement pattern \(D(g)\mathbf u\). The transformation law of the axion angle, Eq.~\ref{eq:chi-theta}, therefore requires
\[
\theta[D(g)\mathbf u]
=\chi_\theta(g)\theta[\mathbf u]
\quad(\mathrm{mod}\ 2\pi).
\]
The reference value \(\theta_0\) obeys the same transformation law because \(g\) is a symmetry of the undistorted crystal. Subtracting the reference relation cancels the fixed \(2\pi\) offset on the continuous branch chosen above, giving
\[
\theta[D(g)\mathbf u]-\theta_0
=\chi_\theta(g)\bigl(\theta[\mathbf u]-\theta_0\bigr).
\]
Retaining the terms linear in the displacements yields
\[
\delta\theta^{(1)}[D(g)\mathbf u]
=\chi_\theta(g)\delta\theta^{(1)}[\mathbf u].
\]

Collecting the atomic axion charges into a vector \(\mathbf Z^\theta\), the linear response is
\[
\delta\theta^{(1)}[\mathbf u]
=(\mathbf Z^\theta)^T\mathbf u.
\]
Since the preceding transformation relation holds for every displacement pattern,
\[
(\mathbf Z^\theta)^T D(g)
=\chi_\theta(g)(\mathbf Z^\theta)^T.
\]
Using the orthogonality of \(D(g)\), this is equivalent to
\[
D(g)\mathbf Z^\theta
=\chi_\theta(g)\mathbf Z^\theta.
\]
The pattern of atomic axion charges therefore transforms according to the one-dimensional representation \(\chi_\theta\).

To connect this result to phonons, we first specify what it means to assign a phonon an irrep. The harmonic equations of motion respect the magnetic symmetries, so applying a symmetry operation to a normal-mode pattern produces another normal-mode pattern with the same frequency. The patterns can therefore be organized into sets that transform among themselves. An irreducible set is one that cannot be separated into smaller sets transforming independently. A one-dimensional irrep contains a single pattern; a multidimensional irrep contains degenerate partner patterns that symmetry can mix.

Consider one such irreducible set and choose orthonormal patterns \(\mathbf v^{(i)}\), with amplitudes \(Q_i\). Within this set,
\[
\mathbf u=\sum_i Q_i\mathbf v^{(i)},
\qquad
\delta\theta^{(1)}=\sum_i\gamma_iQ_i,
\qquad
\gamma_i=\sum_\kappa
\mathbf Z^\theta_\kappa\cdot\mathbf v_\kappa^{(i)}.
\]
Suppose that at least one coefficient \(\gamma_i\) is nonzero. The transformation law of the linear response then requires the amplitude combination
\[
Q_\theta=
\frac{\sum_i\gamma_iQ_i}
{\sqrt{\sum_i\gamma_i^2}}
\]
to transform independently as
\[
Q_\theta\longmapsto\chi_\theta(g)Q_\theta.
\]
Because the symmetry transformations are orthogonal, the remaining amplitude combinations can be chosen orthogonal to \(Q_\theta\) and do not mix with it. A nonzero linear response would therefore separate one independently transforming coordinate from the remaining coordinates. This is compatible with irreducibility only if the set consists of that single coordinate.

Consequently, a phonon can couple linearly to \(\theta\) only if it belongs to the one-dimensional representation with character \(\chi_\theta\). Its displacement pattern must satisfy
\[
D(g)\mathbf v=\chi_\theta(g)\mathbf v
\]
for every magnetic symmetry operation: it changes sign under every \(\theta\)-odd operation and remains unchanged under every \(\theta\)-even operation. The representation may occur several times, allowing several independent phonons with this symmetry. Symmetry permits their linear couplings but does not fix their values or require them to be nonzero.

We consider distortions that are periodic in the magnetic unit cell: atom $\kappa$ of that cell, with mass $m_\kappa$, is displaced by $\mathbf u_\kappa$ in every cell. 



In the magnetic cell, these are zone-center phonons.
With harmonic force constants $\Phi_{\kappa\alpha,\kappa'\beta}=\partial^2E/\partial u_{\kappa\alpha}\partial u_{\kappa'\beta}$, the normal modes solve
\begin{equation}
\sum_{\kappa'\beta}\frac{\Phi_{\kappa\alpha,\kappa'\beta}}{\sqrt{m_\kappa m_{\kappa'}}}\,e_{\kappa'\beta,n}=\omega_n^2\,e_{\kappa\alpha,n},
\qquad
\mathbf u_\kappa=\sum_n\frac{\mathbf e_{\kappa n}}{\sqrt{m_\kappa}}\,q_n,
\eqlab{dynmat}
\end{equation}
with orthonormal eigenvectors $\mathbf e_n$ and normal coordinates $q_n$.
For the symmetry analysis it is convenient to describe a distortion as a pattern $\mathbf v=\{\mathbf v_\kappa\}$ with amplitude $Q$, $\mathbf u_\kappa=Q\,\mathbf v_\kappa$; a normal mode is the pattern $\mathbf v_\kappa=\mathbf e_{\kappa n}/\sqrt{m_\kappa}$ with amplitude $q_n$.
We write $\theta(Q)=\theta_0+\theta'_{\mathbf v}Q+\mathcal O(Q^2)$, with $\theta_0$ the value in the undistorted crystal.
Displacements are time-even; an element $g$ of the magnetic group of the undistorted crystal permutes and rotates the $\mathbf v_\kappa$, and we denote the resulting pattern $g\mathbf v$.

\subsection{The linear selection rule}
\seclab{selection-rule}

An element $g$ of the undistorted group maps the crystal displaced by $Q\mathbf v$
onto the crystal displaced by $Q\,D(g)\mathbf v$, where the $3N\times3N$ matrix
$D(g)$ moves each atom to its image and rotates its displacement.
It also multiplies the small change $\theta-\theta_0$ by $\chi_\theta(g)$.
The slope $\theta'_{\mathbf v}=\partial\theta/\partial Q$ therefore obeys
$\theta'_{D(g)\mathbf v}=\chi_\theta(g)\,\theta'_{\mathbf v}$ for every $g$.
Averaging this relation over the group gives $\theta'_{\mathbf v}=\theta'_{P\mathbf v}$, with
\begin{equation}
P=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\chi_\theta(g)\,D(g).
\eqlab{projector}
\end{equation}
$P$ projects onto the patterns that transform as $\theta$, $D(g)\mathbf v=\chi_\theta(g)\mathbf v$
for all $g$; we denote this symmetry, the one-dimensional representation
$g\mapsto\chi_\theta(g)$, by $\Gamma_\theta$. Hence
\begin{quote}
\emph{A displacement changes $\theta$ at linear order only through its component that transforms exactly as $\theta$.}
\end{quote}
The number of independent such patterns is the trace of the projector,
\begin{equation}
n_\theta=\Tr P=\frac{1}{|G|}\sum_{g\in G}\chi_\theta(g)\,\Tr D(g),
\eqlab{n-theta}
\end{equation}
where $\Tr D(g)$ is the number of atoms that $g$ leaves in place times $\Tr R(g)$.

\subsection{Axion-active phonons are optically dark}

A zone-center mode is infrared-active when it transforms as a component $r_i$ of a polar, time-even vector.
For it to be both infrared- and axion-active, the product $\theta\,r_i$ must contain the identity representation.
But $\theta r_i$ transforms as $s(g)\det R\,R$, which is precisely the transformation law of a magnetization component $M_i$ (axial and time-odd).
At the level of characters, the multiplicity of $\Gamma_\theta$ in the polar-vector representation, $|G|^{-1}\sum_g s(g)\det R\,\Tr R$, is identical to the number of symmetry-allowed components of $\mathbf M$.
Thus:
\begin{quote}
\emph{An axion-active phonon is infrared-active only if the magnetic point group allows a net magnetization along the corresponding direction.}
\end{quote}
As for any phonon, these activities are classified in the group of the undistorted crystal, in which the mode's amplitude transforms according to its irreducible representation; the mode itself necessarily breaks every $\theta$-odd element. If the undistorted group contains unprimed inversion, $\chi_\theta(i)=-1$ makes every axion-active mode odd (ungerade) under it, while every component of the (polar, time-even) Raman tensor is even, so an axion-active mode is never Raman-active. This is the familiar mutual-exclusion rule of centrosymmetric crystals, applied to $\theta$. When $\theta$ is quantized only by other elements, such as unprimed mirrors or primed proper rotations, the rule does not apply, and an axion-active mode can be Raman-active if $\Gamma_\theta$ appears in the representation of symmetric rank-2 tensors.
Both \YIO{} and \MBT{} are compensated magnets with unprimed inversion, so their axion-active phonons are silent to first-order infrared absorption and Raman scattering.
An axion-active phonon drivable by first-order electric-dipole infrared absorption requires a magnet that allows a net moment.
As a control, we repeated the analysis for \MBT{} with ferromagnetic order in the same cell: the axion-active set moves from the antiphase $T_2^-$ modes to their in-phase, $c$-polarized partners, which are infrared-active, exactly as the rule predicts.
In bulk, ferromagnetic \MBT{} is predicted to be a magnetic Weyl semimetal~\cite{li2019intrinsic}, for which the insulating Chern-Simons $\theta$ is not defined; predicted centrosymmetric ferromagnetic axion insulators such as MnBi$_8$Te$_{13}$~\cite{hu2020realization} are natural candidates for infrared-drivable axion phonons.

\subsection{No-go for uniform magnetic fluctuations}

In both materials, each magnetic ion sits at an inversion center that is unprimed in the magnetic group: Ir occupies the $16c$ sites of $Fd\bar3m$, and in \MBT{} inversion about one Mn maps the Mn of the other septuple layer onto a lattice translate of itself in the magnetic cell.
Spins are axial vectors and are unchanged by inversion.
Consequently, \emph{every} spin configuration on these fixed magnetic sites that is periodic in the magnetic cell preserves this inversion.
For insulating states with vanishing bulk Chern numbers, $\theta$ therefore remains quantized to $0$ or $\pi$ and cannot change along a continuous spin deformation that keeps the electronic gap open~\cite{turner2012quantized}.
This includes periodic canting and spin-flop configurations within a connected gapped phase; a gap closing can change the topological phase or make $\theta$ undefined.
Equivalently, \eq{n-theta} gives $n_\theta=0$ for the representation of uniform spin fluctuations in both materials.
For the pyrochlore iridates this restates the known fact that their $q=0$ magnetic orders preserve inversion~\cite{wan2011topological}.
Among the uniform spin and lattice degrees of freedom considered here, inversion-breaking phonons can thus carry a bulk dynamical axion in \MBT{}, while spin fluctuations at fixed ionic positions cannot.
The linear-order obstruction was already noted in Refs.~\cite{zhang2020large, sekine2021axion}: inversion keeps $\theta$ quantized in bulk \MBT{}, so that N\'eel-field fluctuations make no linear contribution to a dynamical axion field.
The result is also consistent with first-principles and model work that turned to films~\cite{zhu2021tunable} or to the related compound Mn$_2$Bi$_2$Te$_5$~\cite{zhang2020large} to obtain a magnon-driven dynamical axion, and with the observation of Ref.~\cite{qiu2025observation}, which was made on an even-layer film where the layer-exchanging symmetry is $\mathcal{PT}$ rather than $\mathcal P$.
The supplement of Ref.~\cite{qiu2025observation} identifies the finite-thickness hybridization gap as essential to that film's dynamical axion.
This film mechanism differs from the phonon coupling computed here, which survives in the bulk; the site-inversion argument does not rule out finite-wave-vector spin modes or inversion-breaking electronic collective excitations.

\subsection{Linear coupling and axion charges}
\seclab{axion-charge}

Consider a periodic displacement of the atoms, $\Delta\mathbf r_\kappa=\lambda\,\mathbf v_\kappa$, where $\kappa$ labels the atoms of the (magnetic) unit cell and the same displacement is applied in every cell.
For small $\lambda$,
\begin{equation}
\theta(\lambda)=\theta_0+\theta'\lambda+\mathcal O(\lambda^2),
\qquad
\theta'=\sum_\kappa\Tth_\kappa\cdot\mathbf v_\kappa,
\eqlab{axion-charge}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{equation}
\Tth_{\kappa\alpha}\equiv\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial u_{\kappa\alpha}},
\eqlab{axion-charge-def}
\end{equation}
where $u_{\kappa\alpha}$ is the Cartesian displacement of sublattice $\kappa$.
We refer to $\Tth_\kappa$ as the \emph{axion charge} of atom $\kappa$, by analogy with the Born effective charge $Z^*_{\kappa,\alpha\beta}=(\Omega/e)\,\partial P_\alpha/\partial u_{\kappa\beta}$ and with the dynamical magnetic charges of Ref.~\cite{ye2014dynamical}.
Because $\theta$ is a pseudoscalar, $\Tth_\kappa$ transforms as an axial, time-odd vector attached to site $\kappa$ (\sref{symmetry}).
A uniform translation cannot change $\theta$, which gives the sum rule $\sum_\kappa\Tth_\kappa=0$.
For a normal mode $n$ with mass-weighted eigenvector $\mathbf e_{\kappa n}$ and normal coordinate $q_n$, $\Delta\mathbf r_\kappa=q_n\mathbf e_{\kappa n}/\sqrt{m_\kappa}$ and
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial q_n}=\sum_\kappa\frac{\Tth_\kappa\cdot\mathbf e_{\kappa n}}{\sqrt{m_\kappa}}.
\eqlab{mode-coupling}
\end{equation}


\subsection{Symmetry constraints on the axion angle}

Certain symmetries constrain $\theta$ to the values $0$ or $\pi$ (mod $2\pi$) \cite{Turner2012, mong2010}.
Specifically, any symmetry that reverses the spatial orientation or reverses time constrains $\theta = -\theta$ (mod $2\pi$), which forces $\theta = 0$ or $\pi$.
This includes:
(i) spatial inversion $\mathcal{P}$;
(ii) time-reversal $\mathcal{T}$;
(iii) any improper spatial rotation (mirror, glide, or roto-inversion); and
(iv) composite antiunitary symmetries such as $\mathcal{T}$ followed by a fractional lattice translation.
In magnetic insulators where $\mathcal{T}$ is broken by spin order, the quantization of $\theta$ can still be enforced by a combination of inversion $\mathcal{P}$ with residual magnetic symmetries.

\subsection{Derivative of the axion angle: four-curvature formulation}

To compute the linear response of $\theta$ to a perturbation parametrized by a scalar amplitude $\lambda$ (such as a phonon displacement), we work in the extended parameter space $(k_x,k_y,k_z,\lambda)$ and evaluate \eq{4curv}.
The Berry curvature in this four-dimensional space is defined as
\begin{equation}
\Omega_{ij} = \partial_i \mathcal{A}_j - \partial_j \mathcal{A}_i - i[\mathcal{A}_i, \mathcal{A}_j],
\eqlab{curvature}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{A}_{i,mn} = \langle u_{m\mathbf{k}} | \partial_i | u_{n\mathbf{k}} \rangle$ is the non-Abelian Berry connection and the indices $i,j$ run over $(k_x, k_y, k_z, \lambda)$.
The trace in \eq{4curv} runs over occupied bands.

In practice, derivatives with respect to the crystal-momentum coordinates $k_x, k_y, k_z$ are evaluated analytically using velocity operators obtained from the tight-binding Hamiltonian, while derivatives with respect to $\lambda$ are computed using finite differences between Hamiltonians evaluated at neighboring displacement amplitudes.
Because the integrand in \eq{4curv} involves only gauge-covariant quantities (Berry curvatures), the result is gauge-invariant and avoids the numerical instabilities associated with direct evaluation of the Chern-Simons 3-form.

\section{Axion-active phonons: general symmetry analysis}
\seclab{symmetry}
%==========================================================================

To induce a first-order change in $\theta$ away from its quantized value, a phonon mode must break \emph{all} symmetries that enforce the quantization $\theta = 0$ or $\pi$.
We refer to such modes as \emph{axion-active phonons}.
More precisely, given the set of crystallographic symmetries $\{g_i\}$ that individually constrain $\theta$ to a quantized value, an axion-active phonon mode is one whose displacement pattern breaks every $g_i$ simultaneously.
If even one protecting symmetry survives the distortion, $\theta$ remains quantized and the first-order response vanishes.

The identification of axion-active modes proceeds in several steps.
First, we determine the protecting symmetries of the undistorted magnetic crystal.
Second, we enumerate the irreducible representations of phonon modes at the relevant wavevectors (which may be at the zone center, zone boundary, or other high-symmetry points, depending on the material and the nature of the protecting symmetries).
Third, for each irreducible representation, we determine which of the protecting symmetries are broken by the corresponding displacement pattern.
Those representations that break all protecting symmetries are the axion-active irreps, and all modes transforming under such irreps are axion-active phonons.

In the two materials studied in this work, the protecting symmetries, the quantized values of $\theta$, and the resulting axion-active irreps all differ qualitatively, illustrating the richness of axion-phonon physics across different classes of magnetic insulators.

\section{MnBi$_2$Te$_4$}
\seclab{MBT}
%==========================================================================

\subsection{Crystal and magnetic structure}

MnBi$_2$Te$_4$ (MBT) crystallizes in a rhombohedral structure with space group $R\bar{3}m$ (No.~166).
The structure consists of septuple layers Te-Bi-Te-Mn-Te-Bi-Te stacked along the crystallographic $c$ axis.
The Mn ions adopt a $2+$ valence with spin $S = 5/2$ and a large magnetic moment of approximately $5\,\mu_\text{B}$.
Below the N\'eel temperature $T_N = 24$~K, the Mn$^{2+}$ moments order ferromagnetically within the $ab$ plane but antiferromagnetically along the $c$ axis in an A-type antiferromagnetic (AFM) arrangement \cite{Yan2019}.

In the magnetically ordered state, time-reversal symmetry $\mathcal{T}$ is broken.
However, the system retains spatial inversion $\mathcal{P}$ (centered at the Mn site) as well as the combined antiunitary symmetry $\Theta = \mathcal{T} S_{1/2}$, consisting of time reversal followed by a half-lattice translation along the $c$ axis \cite{mong2010}.
This half-translation maps Mn$^{2+}$ ions with spin-up to those with spin-down, so that $\Theta$ is preserved despite the magnetic order.
Together, $\mathcal{P}$ and $\Theta$ enforce the quantization $\theta = \pi$, making MBT both an antiferromagnetic topological insulator and an axion insulator \cite{Turner2012}.

At the surface, time-reversal symmetry is broken by the AFM ordering, leading to gapped surface Dirac modes and a half-quantized surface anomalous Hall conductivity.

\subsection{Symmetry analysis and identification of axion-active modes}

To induce a first-order change in $\theta$, a phonon mode must break both $\mathcal{P}$ and $\Theta$.
Because $\Theta$ involves a half-translation along $c$, phonons at finite crystal momentum along $k_z$ are natural candidates for breaking this symmetry.
We therefore focus on phonons at the $Z$ point of the hexagonal Brillouin zone of the non-magnetic crystal structure, corresponding to wavevector $(0,0,\pi/c)$.

Using the \code{SMODES} software package, we enumerate the irreducible representations of the $Z$-point little group and identify displacement patterns that break the relevant symmetries.
Among all irreps at $Z$, the $T_2^-$ representation simultaneously breaks inversion $\mathcal{P}$ and, in the magnetic system, the $\Theta$ symmetry.
There are four distinct modes transforming under $T_2^-$; these are the axion-active phonons of MBT.

The displacement patterns of the four $T_2^-$ modes involve different subsets of atoms in the septuple layer.
Mode~1 is dominated by Mn displacements, mode~2 involves primarily Bi atoms, mode~3 displaces the inner Te atoms, and mode~4 involves the outer Te atoms.
Because these are zone-boundary modes (at $Z$), the atomic displacements alternate in sign between adjacent unit cells along $c$, ensuring that both inversion and $\Theta$ are broken.

\subsection{Computational details for MBT}

The first-principles calculations for MBT are performed using \code{Quantum ESPRESSO} with fully relativistic PAW pseudopotentials incorporating spin-orbit coupling.
The A-type AFM ordering is imposed via noncollinear magnetism, with the Mn$_1$ and Mn$_2$ sublattices initialized with opposite spin orientations along the $c$ axis.
We employ a plane-wave energy cutoff of 60~Ry (charge density cutoff of 240~Ry) and include van der Waals corrections via the DFT-D3 scheme to properly describe the interlayer bonding.
Calculations are performed with and without a Hubbard $U$ correction on the Mn $3d$ orbitals; we test values of $U = 0$, 1, 3, and 4~eV.

For each axion-active phonon mode, atomic positions are displaced along the normalized \code{SMODES} eigenvector with an amplitude $Q = 0.01$~\AA, chosen to remain within the linear-response regime.
Both the undistorted and displaced structures are converged self-consistently, followed by non-self-consistent (NSCF) calculations on a uniform $k$-point mesh.

The resulting Bloch eigenstates are Wannierized using \code{Wannier90} \cite{marzari1997, marzari2012} with a disentanglement window of $-7$ to $15$~eV and a frozen window of $2.5$ to $11$~eV.
The initial projections consist of Mn $3d$, Bi $6p$, and Te $5p$ orbitals on all atoms in the unit cell, yielding 92 spinor trial states that span 46 Wannier functions (after accounting for spin-orbit coupling) from a set of approximately 75 entangled bands.
The Wannierized tight-binding Hamiltonians are imported into the \code{PythTB} framework \cite{Cole_Python_Tight_Binding_2025} for efficient evaluation of the axion response.

An important technical consideration concerns the $\mathcal{P}\Theta$ symmetry, which enforces Kramers-like degeneracy at every $\mathbf{k}$-point even in the magnetically ordered state.
\code{Quantum ESPRESSO} does not automatically detect this composite symmetry; to enforce the correct degeneracy structure we shift the crystallographic origin to the midpoint between the two Mn sublattices.

\subsection{Results for MBT}

The axion response $\partial_Q \theta$ is computed for each of the four $T_2^-$ modes using \eq{4curv}.
We perform a systematic convergence study as a function of $k$-mesh density, evaluating the integral on uniform meshes ranging from $4 \times 4 \times 4$ to $40 \times 40 \times 40$ points.
The minimum direct band gap of MBT is small (approximately 47~meV at the $Z$ point), requiring dense $k$-meshes for convergence.

For mode~2 of the $T_2^-$ irrep (the Bi-dominated mode) at displacement amplitude $Q = 0.01$~\AA, we obtain a converged value of $\partial_Q \theta \approx 10.0$~rad/\AA\ on the finest meshes.
The convergence behavior is smooth, with the result stabilizing beyond approximately $20 \times 20 \times 20$ $k$-points.

\section{Y$_2$Ir$_2$O$_7$}
\seclab{YIO}
%==========================================================================

\subsection{Crystal and magnetic structure}

Y$_2$Ir$_2$O$_7$ (YIO) is a pyrochlore iridate crystallizing in the cubic $Fd\bar{3}m$ space group (No.~227), with conventional lattice parameter $a = 10.19$~\AA\ \cite{wan2011topological, witczak2014correlated}.
The primitive cell contains 22 atoms: 4~Y at the $16d$ Wyckoff position, 4~Ir at $16c$, 2~O at the $8b$ site, and 12~O at the $48f$ site.
The Ir$^{4+}$ ions ($5d^5$, effective total angular momentum $J_\text{eff} = 1/2$) reside on a pyrochlore lattice---a network of corner-sharing tetrahedra---and form corner-sharing IrO$_6$ octahedra with a tilt angle of approximately $53^\circ$.

Below approximately $T_N \approx 150$~K, the Ir moments order in an all-in-all-out (AIAO) magnetic structure, in which the four Ir moments on each tetrahedron point either all toward or all away from the tetrahedral center \cite{wan2011topological, pesin2010mott}.
The AIAO ordering direction on each Ir sublattice points along the local $\langle 111 \rangle$ axis of the pyrochlore lattice.
This magnetic order breaks time-reversal symmetry $\mathcal{T}$ but preserves spatial inversion $\mathcal{P}$, which maps each ``all-in'' tetrahedron to an ``all-out'' tetrahedron and vice versa.
Inversion alone is therefore sufficient to quantize the axion angle to $\theta = 0$ or $\pi$ \cite{Turner2012}.

The phase realized by Y$_2$Ir$_2$O$_7$ with AIAO order depends sensitively on the strength of electronic correlations.
Theoretical calculations predict a Weyl semimetal at weak correlations, a topological axion insulator with $\theta = \pi$ at intermediate correlations, and a trivial Mott insulator with $\theta = 0$ at strong correlations \cite{wan2011topological, witczak2014correlated}.
In this work, we study YIO in a parameter regime (Hubbard $U = 1$--$3$~eV on Ir $5d$) where the system is gapped and in the trivial phase with $\theta = 0$.
This choice provides a non-topological counterpoint to MBT, allowing us to contrast the phonon-induced departure of $\theta$ from $0$ (in YIO) with the departure from $\pi$ (in MBT).
The system exhibits a total magnetization of approximately $2\,\mu_\text{B}$ per formula unit arising from the AIAO-ordered Ir moments.

\subsection{Symmetry analysis and identification of axion-active modes}

In YIO, the axion angle is quantized to $\theta = 0$ solely by spatial inversion $\mathcal{P}$.
An axion-active phonon must therefore break inversion symmetry in order to allow a departure of $\theta$ from zero.
Since $\mathcal{P}$ is a point-group symmetry (with no translational component), it can be broken by zone-center ($\Gamma$-point) phonon modes belonging to irreducible representations that are odd under inversion.

Using the \code{SMODES} software package with the cubic $Fd\bar{3}m$ structure, we enumerate all $\Gamma$-point phonon irreps and their symmetry properties.
The full zone-center phonon representation decomposes as
\begin{equation}
\Gamma_\text{phonon} = A_{1g} \oplus E_g \oplus 2T_{1g} \oplus 4T_{2g} \oplus 3A_{2u} \oplus 3E_u \oplus 8T_{1u} \oplus 4T_{2u},
\eqlab{YIO-decomp}
\end{equation}
where the subscripts $g$ (gerade) and $u$ (ungerade) denote even and odd parity under inversion, respectively.
Only the ungerade representations break $\mathcal{P}$; since the AIAO magnetic order preserves inversion and inversion is the sole protecting symmetry, \emph{any} inversion-odd phonon mode is axion-active.

The ungerade irreps are $A_{2u}$ ($\Gamma_2^-$), $E_u$ ($\Gamma_3^-$), $T_{1u}$ ($\Gamma_4^-$), and $T_{2u}$ ($\Gamma_5^-$).
Among these, only the $T_{1u}$ modes are infrared (IR) active (including the three acoustic translational modes), while the remaining ungerade irreps are optically silent.
The even-parity irreps $A_{1g}$, $E_g$, and $T_{2g}$ are Raman-active; the $T_{1g}$ modes are neither IR- nor Raman-active.

Of particular interest is the $A_{2u}$ ($\Gamma_2^-$) representation, a singly degenerate inversion-odd irrep.
This representation is \emph{neither infrared-active nor Raman-active}: it is a ``silent mode'' that cannot be excited by uniform electric fields or detected by first-order optical spectroscopy.
The \code{SMODES} analysis identifies three distinct $\Gamma_2^-$ modes: one involves displacements of the four Y atoms along their respective local $\langle 111 \rangle$ directions, one displaces the four Ir atoms in an analogous pattern, and one involves the twelve $48f$ oxygen atoms.
In each case, the displacement pattern has the characteristic form of atoms on each sublattice moving along alternating $\langle 111 \rangle$ directions such that inversion symmetry is broken while the cubic point group operations that commute with inversion are preserved.
All three modes are axion-active.

The fact that the $\Gamma_2^-$ mode is optically silent has a significant physical consequence: the axion angle can be modulated by a lattice vibration that is invisible to standard infrared absorption and Raman scattering experiments.
This represents a fundamentally different kind of coupling between the electromagnetic response and lattice degrees of freedom---one that is intrinsically topological in nature and does not manifest in the conventional optical response.

By contrast, the $T_{1u}$ modes are both axion-active and infrared-active, meaning they couple simultaneously to the axion angle and to uniform electric fields.
The $E_u$ and $T_{2u}$ modes are also axion-active but, like $A_{2u}$, are optically silent.

\subsection{Computational details for YIO}

The first-principles calculations for Y$_2$Ir$_2$O$_7$ are performed using \code{Quantum ESPRESSO} with fully relativistic PAW pseudopotentials and spin-orbit coupling.
The AIAO magnetic order is imposed by initializing the four Ir sublattice moments along their respective local $\langle 111 \rangle$ directions: Ir$_1$ along $[\bar{1}\bar{1}\bar{1}]$, Ir$_2$ along $[\bar{1}11]$, Ir$_3$ along $[1\bar{1}1]$, and Ir$_4$ along $[11\bar{1}]$.
The four Ir sites are treated as distinct atomic types in the \code{Quantum ESPRESSO} input to allow independent specification of the magnetic moment directions.
We use a plane-wave energy cutoff of 50~Ry (charge density cutoff of 500~Ry) and a $4 \times 4 \times 4$ $k$-point mesh for the self-consistent calculation.
A Hubbard $U$ correction is applied to the Ir $5d$ states within the ortho-atomic DFT+$U$ framework; we test values of $U = 1.0$ and $3.0$~eV.
Marzari-Vanderbilt cold smearing with a broadening of 5~mRy is used to stabilize the self-consistent cycle.

For each $\Gamma_2^-$ phonon mode, we displace the atomic positions by an amplitude of $Q = 0.01$~\AA\ along the normalized \code{SMODES} eigenvector.
The electronic structure of both undistorted and displaced configurations is then Wannierized and the axion response computed following the same four-curvature procedure used for MBT.

%==========================================================================
\section{Computational methodology}
\seclab{methods}
%==========================================================================

\subsection{Phonon symmetry analysis}

The identification of axion-active phonon modes is performed using the \code{SMODES} software package from the ISOTROPY suite \cite{smodes}, which enumerates the irreducible representations of phonon displacement patterns at specified wavevectors for a given space group and Wyckoff positions.
For MBT, we use the non-magnetic hexagonal $R\bar{3}m$ structure and analyze modes at both the $\Gamma$ and $Z$ points.
For YIO, we use the cubic $Fd\bar{3}m$ structure and analyze modes at the $\Gamma$ point.
In each case, the output provides normalized displacement vectors for each mode within each irrep, which serve as the displacement patterns for the first-principles calculations.

\subsection{Density-functional theory calculations}

All density-functional theory calculations are performed using the \code{Quantum ESPRESSO} package with fully relativistic projector augmented-wave (PAW) pseudopotentials, noncollinear magnetism, and spin-orbit coupling.
The exchange-correlation functional is treated within the PBE generalized gradient approximation.
A Hubbard $U$ correction is applied to the transition-metal $d$ states to account for on-site Coulomb repulsion; the optimal value of $U$ is determined by comparison with experimental band gaps and magnetic moments.

For each material, we perform self-consistent (SCF) calculations for the undistorted structure and for structures displaced along each axion-active phonon mode.
Non-self-consistent (NSCF) calculations are then performed on a uniform $k$-point mesh to obtain the Bloch eigenstates needed for Wannierization.

\subsection{Wannierization}

Starting from the NSCF Bloch eigenstates, we construct maximally localized Wannier functions using \code{Wannier90} \cite{marzari1997, marzari2012, soluyanov2011}.
The disentanglement procedure is employed to extract a set of smoothly connected bands from the entangled manifold.
Initial projections are chosen based on the atomic orbital character of the relevant bands (transition-metal $d$ states, heavy-element $p$ states, etc.), and the resulting tight-binding Hamiltonians faithfully reproduce the low-energy electronic structure.

For MBT, the Wannier basis consists of 92 spinor trial states (Mn $3d$, Bi $6p$, Te $5p$) spanning 46 Wannier functions with a disentanglement window of $[-7, 15]$~eV and a frozen window of $[2.5, 11]$~eV.
The Wannierized Hamiltonians are imported into the \code{PythTB} framework \cite{Cole_Python_Tight_Binding_2025}.

\subsection{Evaluation of the axion response}

The axion response $\partial_\lambda \theta$ is evaluated using \eq{4curv} on the Wannierized tight-binding Hamiltonians.
The four-dimensional Berry curvature $\Omega_{ij}$ in the extended space $(k_x, k_y, k_z, \lambda)$ is constructed as follows.

Derivatives of the Bloch states with respect to crystal momenta $k_\mu$ ($\mu = x,y,z$) are obtained from the velocity operators,
\begin{equation}
v_\mu = \frac{1}{\hbar} \partial_{k_\mu} H(\mathbf{k}),
\eqlab{velocity}
\end{equation}
which are computed analytically from the tight-binding Hamiltonian $H(\mathbf{k})$.
Derivatives with respect to the phonon amplitude $\lambda$ are obtained by finite difference,
\begin{equation}
\partial_\lambda | u_{n\mathbf{k}} \rangle \approx \frac{| u_{n\mathbf{k}}(\lambda + \delta\lambda) \rangle - | u_{n\mathbf{k}}(\lambda - \delta\lambda) \rangle}{2\delta\lambda},
\eqlab{findiff}
\end{equation}
where $\delta\lambda$ corresponds to the phonon displacement amplitude $Q$.
The Berry connections and curvatures in the mixed $(k, \lambda)$ space are then assembled from these ingredients, and the Levi-Civita contraction in \eq{4curv} is evaluated numerically on a uniform 3D $k$-mesh.

An important feature of this approach is that it requires only tight-binding Hamiltonians at two (or more) values of $\lambda$, corresponding to the undistorted and displaced crystal structures.
No perturbation-theory machinery within the DFT code is needed; all quantities are computed from the ground-state electronic structures at each displacement.

\subsection{Convergence considerations}

The convergence of $\partial_\lambda \theta$ with respect to $k$-mesh density is controlled by the minimum band gap of the system: smaller gaps require denser meshes, as the Berry curvature is concentrated near gap-closing points.
For MBT, the minimum direct gap is approximately 47~meV at the $Z$ point, necessitating $k$-meshes of at least $20 \times 20 \times 20$ for reliable convergence.
We perform systematic convergence studies with mesh sizes from $4^3$ to $40^3$ for each mode.

\section{Summary and outlook}
\seclab{conclusions}
%==========================================================================

We have developed a first-principles methodology for identifying and computing the coupling of the Chern-Simons axion angle to lattice vibrations in magnetic insulators.
By combining symmetry analysis with a gauge-invariant four-curvature formulation evaluated on Wannierized tight-binding Hamiltonians, we avoid the gauge ambiguities inherent in the Chern-Simons 3-form and obtain numerically stable results for $\partial_\lambda \theta$.

We have applied this approach to two materials with contrasting axion topology.
In MnBi$_2$Te$_4$, where $\theta = \pi$ is protected by both inversion $\mathcal{P}$ and the combined symmetry $\Theta = \mathcal{T} S_{1/2}$, the axion-active phonons are zone-boundary modes at the $Z$ point belonging to the $T_2^-$ irreducible representation.
These modes must reside at the zone boundary because the $\Theta$ symmetry involves a half-lattice translation, which can only be broken by distortions that modulate at the corresponding wavevector.
For the Bi-dominated $T_2^-$ mode, we find a substantial axion-phonon coupling of $\partial_Q \theta \approx 10$~rad/\AA, representing a departure from $\pi$.

In Y$_2$Ir$_2$O$_7$, studied in a non-topological regime where $\theta = 0$ is quantized solely by inversion in the AIAO magnetic structure, the axion-active phonons are zone-center modes belonging to any inversion-odd irreducible representation.
We have focused on the $\Gamma_2^-$ ($A_{2u}$) representation, a singly degenerate mode that is notable for being \emph{neither infrared-active nor Raman-active}---a ``silent'' modulator of the axion angle.
This illustrates that the axion-phonon coupling can activate degrees of freedom that are invisible to conventional optical probes.

The two materials studied here illustrate the diversity of axion-phonon physics across both topological and trivial magnetic insulators.
In MBT, the axion-active modes are necessarily at the zone boundary due to the translational component of the protecting symmetry and modulate $\theta$ away from $\pi$, while in YIO they are at the zone center, optically silent, and modulate $\theta$ away from $0$.
Comparing the magnitudes and character of the axion response in these two regimes provides insight into how the axion-phonon coupling depends on the underlying band topology.
These results motivate further exploration of axion-phonon coupling across different magnetic insulators, as well as investigations into whether such couplings could be detected through alternative experimental probes such as neutron scattering, hyper-Raman spectroscopy, or nonlinear optical measurements.
