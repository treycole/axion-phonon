## Figure guide: how to read each figure

### Fig. 1, `mbt_dtheta_convergence.png`: MBT k convergence and the Q dependence

**The k mesh ("tube").** ∂θ/∂Q is ∫ c₂ d³k. In MBT, c₂ is large only within ~0.1 Å⁻¹ of the k_z line through Γ,
where the bands are inverted and the gap is ~0.2 eV. So the mesh is uniform along k_z and refined around that line
(`archive/diagnostics/2026-10-19_mbt_refined_bz/make_klist_tube.py`):
- **k_z planes:** the zone is cut into N₃ equally spaced planes along the k_z line. The k_z period is 0.230 Å⁻¹, so
  8 planes (production) are 0.029 Å⁻¹ apart.
  - **Tilt:** each plane is spanned by the in-plane reciprocal vectors b₁, b₂. In the rhombohedral cell these tilt
    slightly out of the xy plane, so k_z varies by ±0.03 Å⁻¹ across a plane.
- **Coarse grid:** within each plane, a 16×16 grid of cells, 0.105 Å⁻¹ on a side.
- **Refined box:** the cells within a Chebyshev distance H of the axis. These are the (2H + 1)² cells around it.
  - **Production:** half-width H = 3, so 7×7 cells. The box edge is 0.317 Å⁻¹ from the axis; this is the dotted
    "refined | coarse" line in Figs. 4 and 5a.
  - **Outside the box:** one k point per coarse cell.
- **In-plane level m:** each box cell is subdivided into an m × m sub-grid.
  - Levels 1, 2, 4, 8 have in-plane spacings 0.105, 0.052, 0.026 and 0.013 Å⁻¹.
  - **Nested:** each level contains the points of the one before.
  - m = 1 means no refinement.
  - **Production:** m = 4, giving 7,928 k points per pair (8 planes × [207 coarse + 49 × 16]).

**Panels (a)–(c): one refinement direction each.** Each line is one basis pattern.
- **y:** the change of the undistorted-structure estimate from the converged value, on a symmetric-log scale. The
  scale is linear inside ±0.1%, which is why ticks jump from 10⁻¹ to 0. The gray band is ±0.2%.
- **(a)** vs the in-plane level, with 16 planes and box half-width 3. Without refinement (m 1), patterns are off by
  tens of percent. m 2 is off by 3–8%, m 4 by ≤ 0.2%, m 8 by ≤ 0.12%.
- **(b)** vs the number of planes, at m 8. 2 and 4 planes are off by 3–10%. 8 → 16 planes changes the result
  ≤ 0.02%.
- **(c)** vs the box half-width, at m 4 and 8 planes. With half-width 2 (5×5 cells) the result is off by up to 0.8%;
  going 3 → 5 changes it ≤ 0.11%.

**Panel (d): pattern 3's two estimates at Q = 0.01 and 0.03 Å.**
- **Dots:** the converged values. **Dashed lines:** fits a + bQ² through each pair of dots.
- **Why a + bQ².** Inversion maps the +Q structure onto the −Q structure and θ − π onto −(θ − π). An estimate made
  from the pair (0, +Q) therefore equals the one from (0, −Q), so each estimate is even in Q: a + bQ² + O(Q⁴), with
  no linear term.
- **What the plot can't show.** Two dots always fit a parabola exactly, so the shape alone proves nothing. The
  evidence is the gap between the two estimates: 0.168 rad/Å at 0.01 Å and 1.615 at 0.03 Å, a ratio of 9.6. Q²
  predicts 9 and a linear term would give 3.
- **Why only the displaced estimate curves.**
  - The two estimates share the secant [H(Q) − H(0)]/Q (and the same for A). They differ in where the bands,
    projectors and curvature are evaluated.
  - The displaced estimate evaluates them in the displaced model, whose tube gap shrinks as Q²: by 3.2 meV at
    0.01 Å and 23.7 meV at 0.03 Å.
  - c₂ grows steeply as the gap closes, and 95% of the gap between the estimates comes from k with a base gap
    < 260 meV. So b is large: +1,780 rad/Å³.
  - The undistorted estimate feels Q only through the secant: b = −27 rad/Å³.
- **Q → 0:** the fits give 6.430 and 6.418 rad/Å (0.2% apart). At Q → 0 the two must coincide exactly.
- **Why compute the displaced estimate at all.**
  - **For a general mode:** both estimates have O(Q) errors of opposite sign, and their mean (the midpoint rule)
    removes them.
  - **For inversion-odd modes** (YIO Γ₂⁻ and MBT T₂⁻), the O(Q) term of the undistorted estimate vanishes by
    symmetry. The undistorted estimate alone is then accurate to O(Q²), and the displaced one serves only as a
    check.
  - **The displaced estimate tracks the slope at the displaced structure.** It grows as the tube gap closes, which
    is real nonlinearity of θ(Q). It is not an error in dθ/dQ at Q = 0.

### Fig. 3, `symmetry_pinned.png`: curvature symmetry per operation

**What is tested.**
- **The test:** a curvature quantity is computed at 7 random (generic) k points, and again at the
  symmetry-transformed points gk. If the model is symmetric, the second set follows from the first by the
  transformation rule.
- **The quantities:**
  - **YIO:** the traced Berry curvature, as a vector (Ω_yz, Ω_zx, Ω_xy). For an operation with rotation R it must
    satisfy Ω(gk) = ±det(R) R Ω(k), with the sign flipped for primed (time-reversing) operations.
  - **MBT:** T_ab = Re Tr[F_a F_b], since PT forces MBT's traced curvature to zero.
- **The relative violation:** max |computed − predicted| over the 7 k and all components, divided by the largest
  |value| of that same part.
- **Each part separately:** each part of the curvature (internal, cross, external) and their sum (total) is tested
  on its own. Each part is normalized by its own magnitude.
  - **So parts are not comparable in absolute size.** In MBT, |T| is 9.4×10⁴ Å⁴ for internal and total, but 3.3×10²
    for cross and 43 for external. A 0.1% external violation is therefore ~5×10⁻⁸ of the total.
- **Floor:** dots at 10⁻⁵ % are exact to machine precision, clipped to fit the log axis.

**Each dot is one symmetry operation of one model** (the identity is left out).

**Horizontal position means nothing.**
- The x axis only groups the dots.
- In (a), each cluster is spread sideways at random so that ~116 dots don't sit on top of each other.
- In (b), the four patterns are placed side by side.
- The dots are therefore not on the tick line by design. The tick marks the cluster and the part name below it marks
  the group. (The first version put one tick between the two clusters, which looked like a missed line; it is now
  labelled per cluster.)

**(a) YIO: base (m-3m′, 47 operations besides the identity) + modes 1–3 (−4′3m′, 23 each); 116 dots per cluster.**
- **Blue vs orange:** the reference positions τ used for the Wannier-Seitz image choice and the embedding.
  - **Blue:** each Wannier function's own centre.
  - **Orange:** the centre pinned to its atom's position in the undistorted structure (production now).
- **Why τ matters:** the total curvature doesn't depend on τ, but its split into internal, cross and external does.
  If the τ's are not exactly symmetry-related, the parts can violate the symmetry while the total doesn't.
  - The Wannier centres sit within 0.045 Å of their atoms but are not exactly symmetry-related.
  - The atoms are.
- **The two blue bands:**
  - The upper band is the operations under which the centres don't map onto each other: 32 of the base's 47 and 16
    of each mode's 23. There, cross is violated by 0.5% and internal by 0.012%.
  - The lower band is the remaining operations. These are presumably the ones under which the centres happen to map
    onto each other; that wasn't checked operation by operation.
- **With pinning (orange):** every operation drops to the lower level.
- **Remaining levels:**
  - internal and total ~10⁻⁴–10⁻³ %: the DFT/Wannier floor (DFT eigenvalues are symmetric to ~1 μeV);
  - cross and external ~10⁻² %: these contain the position matrix A(R), which comes from the `.chk` overlaps.
- **Total:** blue and orange agree, because the total doesn't depend on τ.

**(b) MBT: the four displaced structures (−3′m′, 12 operations), pinned; 10 operations per pattern plotted.**
- **Left out:** the identity, and −1′ (inversion with time reversal). −1′ maps k onto itself and T is PT-even, so it
  is tested trivially.
- **Dots:** −3′m′ operations. **×:** a C4z control, which is not a symmetry; one per pattern.
- **Only 5 operations are independent.** Every primed operation repeats its unprimed partner's test (T is PT-even),
  so the dots overlap in pairs.
- **Floor dots:** the C2x pair (C2x and −C2x′), exact.
- **Raised dots:** the 8 operations that contain a C₃ rotation (C₃, C₃², each alone or times C2x, and their primed
  partners).
  - They show cross 0.018–0.020%, external 0.12–0.14%, total 0.005–0.007%.
  - The same residual appears in the undistorted structure (10-18).
  - Its source within the Wannier model hasn't been isolated.
- **Control:** C4z is violated by 11–30%, so the test can fail.

## 1. Conventions (unchanged from 10-21)

- **Q:** the displacement per Cartesian component along the raw basis pattern (entries ±1).
- **Two estimates:**
  - the **undistorted-structure estimate** ("base end") evaluates the k-space quantities in the undistorted model;
  - the **displaced-structure estimate** ("mode end") evaluates them in the displaced one.
  Both use the same secant ∂_Q H, A.
- **MBT patterns:** Mn, Bi, Te1 (outer, at the van der Waals gap) and Te2 (inner, bonded to Mn). Partner atoms in the
  two septuple layers move oppositely.
- **Models:**
  - A(R) is `transl_inv_full_from_chk`, and H comes from the `.chk` + `.eig` with no hopping cut.
  - The WS tolerance is 5e-3 Å.
  - Positions are atom-pinned (`PIN_TO_ATOMS = True`) for both materials.
  - The undistorted model is the "self-test base": built through the mode pipeline at Q = 0.

- **The 82.7 cm⁻¹ coupling is a near-cancellation between patterns, so it is not determined.** The coupling is

  coupling's magnitude by −21% to +39%).

**Physical picture (added 10-28).**
- **Geometry:** every T₂⁻ pattern and mode is along c, and the two septuple layers (SLs) of the magnetic cell move in
  antiphase. Te1 = outer Te (at the vdW gap), Te2 = inner Te (bonded to Mn).
- **Axion charges, per atom of SL A moving +z (its SL B partner moves −z), in rad/Å:**
  - Cations are negative: Mn −0.99, Bi −0.96.
  - Anions are positive: Te1 +1.61, Te2 +1.14.
  - Cations and anions have opposite signs, as formal valences (Mn²⁺, Bi³⁺, Te²⁻) suggest Born charges would. No Born
    charges have been computed for MBT, so this is an analogy, not a result. The overall sign flips with the AFM
    domain (all moments reversed); only the cation/anion relative sign is meaningful.
- **The modes** (displacements within SL A, normalized to the largest):

  | ω (cm⁻¹) | Mn | Bi | Te1 | Te2 | what moves |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 17.8 | −0.95 | −0.93 | −0.95 | −1.00 | each SL as a rigid block; alternate vdW gaps open and close |
  | 82.7 | −0.91 | +0.35 | +0.69 | −1.00 | MnTe₂ core (Mn + Te2) against the Bi–Te1 shells |
  | 128.7 | −1.00 | −0.25 | +0.33 | +0.28 | cations against anions (Mn rattling in its Te cage) |
  | 143.3 | −1.00 | +0.30 | −0.37 | +0.11 | Mn + Te1 against Bi |

- **Rule:** ∂θ/∂q = Σ_κ z_κ u_κ.
  - The coupling is largest when cations move against anions (128.7: all four terms positive).
  - It nearly vanishes when each cation moves against the other cation and each anion against the other anion
    (82.7: Mn against Bi, Te2 against Te1).
- **Why antiphase:**
  - The distortion makes each SL polar along z, with the sign alternating from SL to SL, as the Mn moments do.
  - So P_z·M_z is the same in every SL, a uniform magnetoelectric pseudoscalar, which shifts θ.
  - There is no net dipole, so the modes are IR-silent. With FM order the in-phase modes take this role (main2.tex, the FM control in the
    selection-rule section).
- **Interlayer part:**
  - The axion charges of one SL sum to +2.58 rad/Å.
  - A rigid shift of an isolated SL would change nothing, so the rigid-layer mode (17.8) couples entirely through
    the interlayer (vdW) coupling.
  - Its zero-point δθ (0.12 rad) is as large as that of the 128.7 mode because its zero-point amplitude is large.
- **Band edges at Γ** (base model 214301, `archive/diagnostics/2026-10-22_mbt_mode_couplings/band_edge_character.py`):
  - The top valence pair is Bi 0.37, Te1 0.53, Te2 0.10, Mn 0.00.
  - The bottom conduction pair is Bi 0.43, Te1 0.29, Te2 0.28, Mn 0.00.
  - The gap states sit on Bi and the outer Te1, at the vdW gap. This fits Te1 having the largest axion charge and the
    sizeable interlayer part.
  - Mn has no weight at the gap and acts through exchange via its Te2 neighbours.
- **Heuristic, untested:** the layer-dipole picture predicts z_κ ∝ Z*_zz,κ. Testing it needs Born charges, i.e. new
  in-phase (Γ) displaced SCFs with Berry-phase polarization; the antiphase T₂⁻ runs have no net P by symmetry.

## 3. MBT: O(Q²) differences between the estimates, and the Q → 0 limit

**Fig. 1(d)** plots both estimates for pattern 3 at Q = 0.01 and 0.03 Å, with fits of the form a + bQ².
- **Undistorted-structure estimate:** 6.427 → 6.406. The fit gives 6.430 at Q → 0.
- **Displaced-structure estimate:** 6.596 → 8.020. Their difference grows 9.6× from 0.01 to 0.03 Å, as expected
  for Q². The fit gives 6.418 at Q → 0.

**Why the difference is O(Q²).**
- T₂⁻ is parity-odd: inversion maps Q → −Q and θ → −θ.
- So θ − π is odd in Q, and dθ/dQ and every gap are even in Q.
- Both estimates therefore differ from the Q → 0 value at O(Q²), not O(Q).

**Why the displaced estimate is the one that moves.**
- Pattern 3 closes the tube gap the most: by 3.2 meV at Q = 0.01 Å and 23.7 meV at 0.03 Å.
- 95% of the difference between the estimates comes from k points where the base gap is < 260 meV.

**Consequence for the paper.**
- It reports the undistorted-structure estimate at 0.01 Å. For pattern 3 this is within 0.05% of the Q → 0 limit.
- The methods text now says both estimates are O(Q²) for an axion-active pattern. Before, it said "differ at O(Q);
  report the mean".

- **The frozen top matters below VBM + 3 eV.** The changes are large, not monotonic, and don't follow Ω_I.
- **Every set agrees on the mesh.** Each window holds every occupied band in its frozen window, so the models give
  the same bands at the 8³ mesh points.
- **So the spread is off-mesh interpolation in the tube.** This fits last week's open band item: near Γ, off the
  k_z line, the gap-edge bands are off by up to 10 meV between mesh points.

**What this means for the production result.**
- Production sits on the plateau.
- The plateau is narrower than YIO's, which spans frozen tops VBM + 0.34 to 2.68 eV at ≤ 0.75%.
- What a plateau shows here is stability, not convergence. The mesh test decides which.
